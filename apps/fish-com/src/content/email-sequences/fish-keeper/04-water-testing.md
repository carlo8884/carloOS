When something goes wrong in an aquarium — sudden deaths, sick fish, algae blooms, fish that won't eat — the first diagnostic step is almost always a water test. Without one, you are guessing. With one, you have actual numbers to compare against a target range, and the problem usually identifies itself.

---

**The reference test kit**

The API Master Test Kit (or equivalent liquid-drop kits) tests the five parameters that drive almost every aquarium problem:

- **Ammonia (NH₃ / NH₄⁺)** — should be 0 in a cycled tank. Any detectable reading means the cycle has crashed or the bioload exceeded the filter's capacity.
- **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)** — should be 0 in a cycled tank. Detectable nitrite has the same meaning as detectable ammonia.
- **Nitrate (NO₃⁻)** — the end-product of the cycle. Target depends on tank type: freshwater fish-only <40 ppm; planted tanks <20 ppm preferred; reef tanks <10 ppm preferred.
- **pH** — target depends on stocking; community freshwater 6.8–7.6; African cichlids 7.8–8.4; marine 8.1–8.4.
- **General hardness (GH) and carbonate hardness (KH)** — for tanks with specific parameter requirements; for stable pH KH is the more important of the two (KH buffers against pH crash).

**Strip tests are too imprecise for diagnosis.** They are useful as a quick screen ("is everything in the right ballpark?") but should not be the basis for any decision. The liquid drop tests cost ~$30 once and last for hundreds of tests over years.

---

**The testing cadence**

- **New tank, first 4–8 weeks (cycling):** test ammonia and nitrite daily; nitrate weekly. Watch the cycle complete.
- **Established tank, first 90 days of stocking:** test all five parameters weekly. You are still learning your tank's specific equilibrium.
- **Mature stable tank:** test ammonia, nitrite, nitrate every 2–4 weeks. Test pH after any water change to confirm stability.
- **Any problem (sick fish, sudden death, behaviour change, algae bloom):** test all five immediately. Almost every problem has a water-quality component.

---

**Reading the results — common patterns**

- **Ammonia detectable, nitrite zero, nitrate stable** — bioload exceeded filter capacity or filter media disturbed (recent cleaning, recent move). Reduce feeding, water change, look for dead fish or rotting plant material.
- **Ammonia zero, nitrite detectable** — partial cycle crash; the Nitrosomonas are working, the Nitrobacter / Nitrospira are not. Water change to dilute, reduce feeding, wait.
- **Nitrate climbing month over month** — water-change cadence inadequate for the stocking. Increase water-change frequency or volume.
- **pH falling over time** — KH being depleted; common in soft-water tanks with active denitrification. Add buffer (crushed coral, alkalinity supplement) or larger / more frequent water changes.
- **Everything reads normal but fish are sick** — water-quality is fine; the problem is parasites, bacterial infection, internal disease, or environmental (temperature swing, recent stress). Look elsewhere; do not over-treat with water-change interventions.

---

**What water testing does not diagnose**

- External parasites (Ich, velvet, flukes) — visible on the fish.
- Bacterial infections — fin rot, mouth rot, internal bacterial disease.
- Internal parasites — long-term stress markers.
- Species-incompatibility stress.

Water testing is the entry point — if water is fine, look at the next category. If water is off, fix that first before any treatment, because most disease responds best on top of correct water quality.

Most new-aquarist fish deaths are not from disease, predator, or bad luck. They are from skipping or rushing the nitrogen cycle. The cycle is the foundation of aquarium-keeping, and it is the single decision that separates a tank that lives for years from a tank whose stock crashes in the second or third week.

---

**What the cycle actually is**

Fish excrete ammonia (NH₃ / NH₄⁺) through their gills and waste. Ammonia is toxic to fish at very low concentrations — chronic exposure damages gill tissue, acute exposure kills outright. A healthy aquarium does not let ammonia accumulate because two populations of beneficial bacteria break it down:

1. **Nitrosomonas** — bacteria that convert ammonia to **nitrite** (NO₂⁻). Nitrite is also toxic to fish.
2. **Nitrobacter and Nitrospira** — bacteria that convert nitrite to **nitrate** (NO₃⁻). Nitrate is far less toxic; it accumulates slowly and is removed by water changes and (in planted tanks) by plant uptake.

Both bacteria populations grow on solid surfaces — the filter media, the substrate, the décor. The "cycle" is the 3–6 week process of growing these bacterial populations to the point where they keep ammonia and nitrite at zero on an ongoing basis.

---

**Two ways to cycle a new tank**

**Fishless cycling (recommended)** — add an ammonia source (pure household ammonia at known concentration, or a commercial ammonia source) to dose the empty tank to ~2 ppm ammonia. Test daily. The cycle is complete when:

1. Ammonia added in the morning reads 0 by the next morning (Nitrosomonas working).
2. Nitrite reads 0 (Nitrobacter / Nitrospira working).
3. Nitrate reads >5 ppm (the end-product of the cycle).

A fishless cycle typically takes 3–6 weeks. No fish are subjected to the toxic intermediate stage. This is the cleanest approach for any new tank.

**Fish-in cycling (commonly forced when the fish are already in the tank)** — using hardy fish (and a heavy reduction in feeding + frequent partial water changes) to drive the bacterial populations to grow without poisoning the residents. Higher risk to the fish, faster route to having fish in the tank, possible to do humanely with care. Default to fishless when starting fresh.

---

**What the test kit is for during cycling**

A liquid drop-test kit (API Master Test Kit is the reference; strip tests are too imprecise for cycling) tells you which stage of the cycle the tank is in:

- **Ammonia rising, nitrite zero, nitrate zero** — bacteria not established yet, ammonia source needs to keep being dosed.
- **Ammonia falling toward zero, nitrite rising** — Nitrosomonas working; Nitrobacter / Nitrospira not yet established.
- **Ammonia zero, nitrite falling toward zero, nitrate rising** — both populations working; cycle nearing completion.
- **Ammonia zero, nitrite zero, nitrate >5 ppm** — cycled. Tank ready for fish.

A tank with detectable ammonia or nitrite is not cycled. Don't add fish to it.

---

**Speeding the cycle up (legitimate ways)**

- **Seed material from an established tank** — used filter media, gravel, or a sponge filter from a friend's cycled aquarium. The seeded bacteria can reduce a cycle from 4 weeks to 1–2 weeks.
- **Bottled bacteria** (Tetra SafeStart, Seachem Stability, Dr. Tim's One & Only) — varies in effectiveness; the live-culture products work better than the dormant-spore products.

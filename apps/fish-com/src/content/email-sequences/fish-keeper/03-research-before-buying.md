The aquarium hobby has more impulse purchases than almost any other livestock-keeping practice — a fish at the store looks small, looks beautiful, and the store associate often doesn't volunteer information that would discourage the sale. Five minutes of research before the fish goes into the bag is the cheapest insurance in the hobby.

Five questions to answer for any species before purchase.

---

**1. What is the adult size?**

Many fish are sold at juvenile size and reach adult sizes the buyer didn't plan for. A common pleco at 3 inches in the store reaches 18+ inches as an adult and needs a 100+ gallon tank to be comfortable. Bala sharks, iridescent sharks, red-tailed black sharks, oscars, silver dollars, many tetras and catfish — all are commonly sold at juvenile size that misleads on adult requirements.

**Check the adult size and the AqAdvisor / SeriouslyFish recommended tank size before purchase, not after.**

---

**2. What are the water-parameter requirements?**

- pH range, hardness (GH/KH), temperature.
- Soft acidic species (Discus, German blue rams, many South American tetras) do not thrive in hard alkaline tap water without modification.
- African Rift Lake cichlids (Mbuna, Tanganyikans) require hard alkaline water; they fail in soft acidic water.
- Marine species require stable salinity (~1.024–1.026 SG), stable temperature, and good alkalinity / calcium / magnesium balance.

Test your tap water before buying any species with specific parameter requirements. Buying a species your water can't support is the most common preventable cause of livestock loss.

---

**3. What is the temperament and compatibility profile?**

- Some species are aggressive (most cichlids, many gouramis as adults, tiger barbs in groups under 6) and will harass smaller or peaceful tankmates.
- Some species are peaceful but easily stressed by aggressive tankmates (Discus, German blue rams, many gouramis).
- Some species require specific group sizes — schooling fish kept in pairs or trios fail (chronic stress, shortened lifespan); the typical minimum is 6+ for most tetras, danios, rasboras, and corydoras.

The "community tank" label on a store sign means little. Cross-check the specific species' temperament and group requirements.

---

**4. What is the diet requirement?**

- Most community fish accept commercial flakes / pellets and frozen foods.
- Carnivores (Discus, larger cichlids, predator species) require meat-based foods and reject pure plant-based formulations.
- Herbivores (plecos, mbuna cichlids, some catfish) require algae- and vegetable-based diets; long-term meat-heavy feeding causes liver damage.
- Some species have specialised requirements (puffers requiring snail-based foods for tooth wear; some catfish requiring sinking foods).

---

**5. What is the lifespan, and are you willing to commit?**

- A common goldfish well-kept lives 10–20 years. A koi can live 25–40 years.
- Many cichlids live 8–15 years.
- A betta lives 3–5 years; many community tetras 5–8 years.
- Marine species vary widely; some clownfish live 20+ years.

The fish you buy today is a multi-year commitment in most cases. Confirm you want that commitment before purchase.

---

**The research tools that work**

- **SeriouslyFish.com** — encyclopaedia-style species profiles with parameter ranges and behaviour notes. Best free reference for freshwater.
- **AqAdvisor.com** — community-driven stocking calculator; gives a rough estimate of whether a planned stocking will work in a specific tank.
- **Reef2Reef forums** — for marine and reef species.

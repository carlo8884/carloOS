A reader named Mark sent us a 90-day log from his first reef tank. With his permission we published it; the condensed timeline is below.

**Day 0.** 40-gallon tank, dry rock, fresh saltwater mixed to 1.025 SG. Dosed pure ammonia to 2 ppm. Heater 78 °F. Lights off for the first two weeks.

**Day 4.** Ammonia at 1.5 ppm. Nitrite at 0.

**Day 8.** Ammonia at 0.5. Nitrite at 2.0. Re-dosed ammonia to 2 ppm.

**Day 14.** Ammonia processing in under 24 hours. Nitrite at 5+. Mark almost added fish. He didn't. (Correct decision.)

**Day 22.** Nitrite still 5+. Tested KH: 4 dKH and dropping. Added 1 tsp baking soda per 10 gal. Nitrite started moving the next day.

**Day 28.** Both ammonia and nitrite to 0 within 24 hours of dosing. Tank cycled.

**Day 30.** Lights on. First diatom bloom (the brown dust on the rocks). This is normal; nutrient pulse before the cleanup crew is in.

**Day 35.** Added cleanup crew — hermits, snails, a small conch. Diatoms gone in five days.

**Day 45.** First fish: a single ocellaris clown. Quarantined separately for 14 days first. (This is the step most beginners skip and the reason most reef tanks crash at the 60-day mark — uninspected fish bring ich and velvet.)

**Day 90.** Three fish, six coral frags, parameters stable, no crashes. He still tests weekly.

What Mark said in the writeup: "Patience is the only skill. Every disaster I've seen on the forum was someone refusing to wait."

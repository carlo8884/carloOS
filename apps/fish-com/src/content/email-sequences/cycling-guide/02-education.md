The single most common email we get during cycling: "Ammonia dropped fine. Nitrite has been at 5+ ppm for two weeks. It won't move."

The PDF tells you to wait. This email tells you why — and the three things that actually unstick it.

**Background.** Cycling is two bacterial colonies, not one. *Nitrosomonas* converts ammonia → nitrite. *Nitrobacter* / *Nitrospira* converts nitrite → nitrate. The second colony grows roughly 3× slower than the first. The nitrite stall is normal. A stall longer than 14 days usually isn't.

**The three actual causes, in order of frequency:**

**1. pH crash.** As the cycle progresses, the bacterial colonies consume carbonate hardness (KH). Below pH ~6.5, the bacteria slow down dramatically. Below 6.0 they essentially stop. If your stall has been long, test KH and pH first. If KH is below 3 dKH, dose a half-teaspoon of baking soda per 10 gallons and re-test in 4 hours.

**2. Insufficient ammonia source.** A lot of cyclers underdose, then panic about high nitrite and stop dosing ammonia. The nitrite-eaters need a continuous nitrite supply — which requires a continuous ammonia supply. Keep dosing ammonia back up to 2 ppm daily even while nitrite is high.

**3. Chlorine in the top-off water.** If you've been topping off with untreated tap, the chlorine is killing the colony as it forms. Dechlorinate every drop of water that touches the tank.

**The one product you actually need to diagnose this** is an accurate liquid test kit. Strips are too imprecise for cycling — they round to half a ppm in a range where 0.25 ppm matters.

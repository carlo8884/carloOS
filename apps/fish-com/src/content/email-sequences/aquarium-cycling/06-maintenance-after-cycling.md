The tank is cycled. The fish are in. From here, the job is **steady, small, weekly maintenance** — not occasional heroic interventions. The goal is to keep conditions stable, because stability is what fish are adapted to. Sudden swings are what stresses and kills them, even when the new condition is "objectively better".

**Water changes: 10–25% weekly.**

Replace 10–25% of the tank's water with dechlorinated tap (or RO + remineralised) water every week. The reason this range and not more:

- A 10–25% change dilutes accumulated nitrate, replenishes trace minerals consumed by fish and plants, and removes dissolved organics — without abruptly changing the water chemistry the fish are acclimated to.
- A 50%+ change can cause an osmotic shock, a temperature mismatch (most people don't temperature-match a 50% change), and a pH/GH/KH swing if your tap water differs meaningfully from the established tank water. Large changes are reserved for **specific** problems (a nitrate spike, an ammonia bloom from overstocking) — not routine.
- The water you replace should be **temperature-matched** by feel (cool/warm side of lukewarm is fine for tropicals; aim within 2 °C of tank temp) and **always dechlorinated** before it hits the tank. Chlorine and chloramine both nuke your filter bacteria.

**Gravel vac: every other week is usually enough.**

A python-style gravel vacuum on the substrate during your weekly change handles the visible debris. Vacuum **half** the substrate each session and alternate halves week-to-week — this preserves the beneficial bacteria in the substrate while still removing waste. In planted tanks, skip the gravel vac under the plant root zone and just surface-skim the open areas; the plant roots benefit from the mulm.

**Filter media: replace far less than the packaging tells you.**

This is the most common rookie expense and the most common rookie mistake. Filter cartridges come with instructions to "replace monthly" — that is marketing, not biology. Your nitrifying bacteria live primarily in and on the filter media. Throwing out the media throws out your cycle.

- **Sponge media:** rinse in tank water (never tap — chlorine kills the bacteria) when flow noticeably drops. Replace only when the sponge is physically falling apart, typically 1–3 years.
- **Ceramic/biological media:** rinse occasionally in tank water, replace essentially never.
- **Carbon:** optional. Removes dissolved organics and medications. Functionally inert after 3–4 weeks. If you use it, swap it monthly. Most established tanks don't need it routinely.
- **Filter floss / fine polishing pads:** the one component genuinely meant to be replaced regularly. Swap monthly or when it discolours.

The rule of thumb: **never replace more than one piece of media at a time, and never simultaneously do a media swap and a large water change.** Stagger anything that disturbs the bacterial colony.

**Test schedule once the tank is settled.**

Daily for the first two weeks after stocking. Weekly for the next month. Then a check every 2–4 weeks indefinitely, plus any time the fish look off, you've added livestock, or you've changed feeding or filtration. Keep the kit on hand — the reagents are good for ~3 years from the manufacture date stamped on the bottle.

**Feeding, briefly.** Most fish are overfed. Feed what the fish eat in 1–2 minutes, once or twice a day, with a one-day fast per week for adult tropicals. Excess food = excess ammonia = stressed bacteria.

**This week:** set a recurring weekly reminder. Same day, same approximate time. Make it boring. Boring is the goal.

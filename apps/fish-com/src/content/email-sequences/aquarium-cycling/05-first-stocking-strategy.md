Your tank is cycled. The 24-hour re-dose test passed twice. Time to stock — carefully.

**Rule one: light load for the first two weeks.**

A cycled bacterial colony is sized to the ammonia it has been processing — in your case, the daily 2 ppm dose. A full stock of fish dropped in on day one produces dramatically more waste than that, and the colony needs time to scale. Stock to roughly **one-third of the tank's eventual capacity** in week one, then add the rest in groups of two or three over the following 2–4 weeks. Test daily during this period and watch for any ammonia or nitrite spike — if either appears, a 30–50% water change buys time while the colony catches up.

**Rule two: pick hardy starters.**

The first fish into a freshly cycled tank takes the brunt of any colony lag. Pick species that tolerate the small bumps without dying.

- **Zebra danio (***Danio rerio***).** Cold-tolerant, fast, shoaling. Active at the top of the tank. Wants a group of 6+. Adult size ~5 cm. One of the most thoroughly studied freshwater fish (it's a major lab organism), well-understood husbandry.
- **White cloud mountain minnow (***Tanichthys albonubes***).** Coolwater tolerant — works in unheated tanks down to ~18 °C. Peaceful, shoaling, easy. Wants a group of 6+. Adult size ~4 cm.
- **Platy (***Xiphophorus maculatus***).** Livebearer, peaceful, colourful. Tolerates a wide pH range. One of the few livebearers genuinely beginner-appropriate. Mind: livebearers breed prolifically — start with same-sex groups if you don't want to manage fry.
- **Bronze or peppered corydoras (***Corydoras aeneus / paleatus***).** Bottom-dwelling, shoaling, peaceful. Wants a group of 6+, sand or smooth gravel substrate, soft hiding places. Eats sinking pellets and leftover food.

**What not to put in first.**

- **Goldfish in a tropical community.** Goldfish are coldwater fish (ideal range ~15–22 °C / 59–72 °F) that produce more waste per body length than nearly any other freshwater species, get large (15–30+ cm depending on variety), and are not compatible with tropical community fish. They belong in their own dedicated tank, ideally 75+ litres for a single fancy goldfish, larger for commons.
- **Bettas as the test fish.** A betta is fine as a stand-alone in a 20-litre+ heated tank, but they are territorial and not a "let's see if the tank is okay" species.
- **Anything bought "to use up the nitrites".** No fish is sacrificial. If your reads are not 0/0/measurable, don't stock.
- **Plecos for "algae control".** The common pleco grows to 45+ cm and outproduces waste like a goldfish. If you want an algae eater, look at otocinclus or nerite snails — but solve the algae cause first, which is light + nutrient balance, not livestock.

**Drip acclimation, briefly.**

Float the sealed bag for 15 minutes to match temperature. Open and move the fish + bag water into a clean container. Start a slow drip from the tank using airline tubing tied in a knot or a $4 acclimation clamp — target ~2 drips per second. Triple the original water volume over 30–45 minutes. Net the fish into the tank. Do not pour bag water into the tank.

**This week:** if the re-dose test has passed twice, pick one shoaling species, buy 5–6 individuals, drip-acclimate, and add them. Test daily for two weeks before adding anything else.

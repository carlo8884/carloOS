Final email in the series.

If you've followed along, your tank has been cycled for about a month and stocked for two or three weeks. The numbers are stable: 0 ammonia, 0 nitrite, nitrate climbing on a weekly schedule that the weekly water change knocks back down. The fish are eating and acting normally. That's the whole job.

**What you've learned, in one paragraph.** Cycling is the process of growing two bacterial colonies — ammonia-oxidisers first, nitrite-oxidisers second — on your filter media until they can process the waste of your eventual fish load. It takes 4–6 weeks. You feed it ammonia (ideally from a bottled aqueous source) to 2 ppm and re-dose as it drops. Nitrite spikes in the middle and is the normal shape of the curve, not a problem. You confirm completion with a 24-hour re-dose test passed twice. You stock light, with hardy shoaling fish, drip-acclimated. You maintain with 10–25% weekly water changes, alternating-half gravel vacs, and you don't throw out filter media. That is the whole hobby's foundation. Everything else is decoration.

**One last thing.** You will come across confident-sounding aquarium advice online — particularly on social media — that contradicts everything in this course. "Cycle in three days with this product." "Goldfish are fine with tropical fish." "Add the fish in week one, that's how the tank cycles itself." Cycling is a biological process governed by bacterial growth kinetics. There is no shortcut to growing a colony. The hobby has decades of experimental and observational evidence on what works; the slow methods are slow because biology is slow. When in doubt, the source is the standard — not the volume.

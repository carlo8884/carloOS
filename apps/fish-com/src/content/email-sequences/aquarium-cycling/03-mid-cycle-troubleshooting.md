Around the two-week mark, the typical fishless cycle hits the wall: ammonia is dropping (good), nitrite is climbing — often off the top of the chart — and nitrate is barely registering. The chart says "stalled". The reality is almost always "in progress".

**What's happening.** Two different bacterial groups are involved. Ammonia-oxidisers (AOB) — primarily *Nitrosomonas* species — establish first; they convert NH3 to NO2. Nitrite-oxidisers (NOB) — in aquaria, mostly *Nitrospira* — establish second; they convert NO2 to NO3. NOB grow more slowly than AOB. That's why you see ammonia drop weeks before nitrite drops. The "nitrite stall" is not a problem — it's the normal shape of the curve.

**What "stalled" actually looks like, and what to do.**

| Reading pattern | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonia 0, nitrite high (>5 ppm), nitrate climbing slowly | Normal mid-cycle | Wait. Re-dose ammonia only when it tests 0. |
| Ammonia 0, nitrite **off the chart**, no nitrate movement for 5+ days | Nitrite may be inhibiting itself | 30–50% water change with dechlorinated water. Retest. Resume daily testing. |
| Ammonia not dropping after 7 days of dosing | AOB never established | Check pH (cycle stalls below ~6.5) and temperature (NOB prefer 25–30 °C / 77–86 °F). |
| Everything stuck for 10+ days | Possibly a chlorinated or chloraminated tap-water reset | Always treat fresh water with a dechlorinator that handles chloramine. |

**The "5+ ppm nitrite plateau" trick.** When nitrite is genuinely off the chart (test cards typically max out around 5 ppm; real value could be 10 or 20+), the bacteria can be inhibited by their own substrate. A 30–50% water change brings nitrite back into a range where the NOB colony can actually grow against it. Re-dose ammonia to 2 ppm afterward if needed. This is the single most common rescue.

**What not to do.**

- Don't add "starter bacteria" products on top of the stalled tank in panic. Most contain the wrong species (older formulations were *Nitrobacter*-based, which isn't the dominant aquarium NOB). The exceptions worth using up front — Tetra SafeStart, Dr. Tim's One & Only, Fritz TurboStart 700 — refrigerated, in-date *Nitrospira*-containing products. If you didn't use one at the start, adding one mid-cycle now mostly resets your timing rather than rescuing it.
- Don't dump in extra ammonia "to feed the bacteria more". You'll inhibit the NOB further.
- Don't add fish to "speed it up". You'll kill them.

**This week:** if nitrite has been pegged for several days with no nitrate movement, do a single 30–50% water change. Otherwise, **leave the tank alone** and keep testing. Patience is the active ingredient.

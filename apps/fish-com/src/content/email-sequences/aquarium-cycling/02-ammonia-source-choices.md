In a fishless cycle, you are deliberately adding ammonia to feed the bacterial colony you're trying to grow. The source you pick determines whether the cycle is clean or contaminated.

**Three categories, only one of them is unambiguously good.**

**1. Pure aqueous ammonia (recommended).** Sold for the hobby under names like Dr. Tim's Ammonium Chloride and Fritz Fishless Fuel. These are precisely-dosed, surfactant-free, fragrance-free ammonia or ammonium chloride solutions. You measure drops or ml per gallon to a target concentration (typically 2 ppm) and re-dose as the bacteria consume it. Predictable, clean, fast. This is the default.

**2. Household ammonia (use with caution).** If you can find it, plain "clear" janitorial-grade ammonia with **no surfactants, no perfumes, no soaps, no detergents** on the label can work. Shake the bottle: if it foams, it has surfactants and is not safe — surfactants disrupt the gill mucosa of fish you'll add later. Strength varies (commonly 5–10%), so you'll dose by experiment, testing after each addition. Generally not worth the risk unless the hobby-grade options aren't available.

**3. Ghost feeding (slow and unreliable).** Adding fish food to an empty tank and letting it decompose to generate ammonia "naturally". The pitfalls are real: ammonia release is gradual and uneven, the decaying food creates a heterotrophic bacterial bloom that competes with the nitrifying bacteria you actually want, and the tank can develop a film of biofilm and fungus on the food itself. Cycles started this way often take 6–10 weeks instead of 4–6. Skip it.

**The 2 ppm target, and why.**

Dose to roughly **2 ppm ammonia** at the start, and re-dose to 2 ppm whenever your daily test reads 0 (which won't happen for the first week or two). Higher concentrations — 4 to 8 ppm — used to be common advice but can actually stall the cycle by inhibiting the nitrite-oxidising bacteria (Nitrobacter / Nitrospira spp.), which are more sensitive to high ammonia than the ammonia-oxidisers. 2 ppm gives the colony enough to grow without poisoning the second half of the pipeline.

**This week:** if you started with ghost feeding or are unsure of your ammonia source, switch to a bottled aqueous ammonia product. Re-dose to 2 ppm. Resume daily testing. Don't be alarmed when nitrite shows up — that's email #3.

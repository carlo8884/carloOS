The schedule tells you "install UVB on day 1." This email tells you what UVB actually is, what the right number is, and how to know whether your setup is hitting it.

**The number that matters is UVI (UV Index)**, not the bulb's labelled percentage. The labels (5.0, 10.0, 12%) describe bulb output. What the animal experiences is UVI at basking distance, which is a function of bulb output, distance from the bulb, fixture reflector, and any mesh between bulb and animal.

**Target UVI by species (Ferguson zones, peer-reviewed reference):**

- **Bearded dragon:** UVI 3.0–4.0 at basking, full-spectrum exposure (Ferguson Zone 3–4).
- **Leopard gecko:** UVI 0.5–1.0 at basking — they're crepuscular and don't need a beardie-level exposure, but recent literature supports low-level UVB.
- **Crested gecko:** UVI 0.5–1.5; low-level UVB benefits demonstrated in studies (de Vosjoli et al.).
- **Ball python:** UVI 0.5–1.0 — historically considered optional, increasingly considered beneficial.

**Two failure modes that the Solarmeter catches:**

1. **Mesh attenuation.** A bulb installed *above* fine mesh loses 30–40% of UVB output. Mount inside the enclosure or use a coarse mesh.
2. **Bulb decay.** A T5 high-output bulb keeps emitting visible light long after UVB output has dropped below target. Without a meter, you can't tell. With one, you replace at month 11–12 like clockwork.

The schedule covers the first 12 months for the four most-kept beginner species — bearded dragon, leopard gecko, crested gecko, and ball python — with the husbandry checkpoints each one needs at each month. The columns are: target ambient and basking temps, UVB bulb installation and replacement dates, feeding schedule changes by month, expected sheds, expected growth markers, and the vet checkpoint months.

Three principles up front, because they will save you a lot of grief:

1. **UVB is non-negotiable for diurnal species.** A bearded dragon without UVB is a bearded dragon developing metabolic bone disease (MBD) on a clock. A T5 high-output bulb (Arcadia 12% or Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 10.0) is the standard. Replace it every 12 months *regardless of whether it still emits visible light* — UVB output drops well before the visible spectrum fails.

2. **Temperature gradients beat thermostats fighting heat lamps.** The animal needs a hot end and a cool end. A single-temperature enclosure is a stressed animal.

3. **Quarantine new animals for 60–90 days minimum, in a separate room.** Crypto, adenovirus, mites — none of these announce themselves on day one.

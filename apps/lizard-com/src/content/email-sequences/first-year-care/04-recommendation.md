After years of reading "what to buy for my first reptile" threads, here's the kit that survives the first year without being replaced. Specified for a bearded dragon setup; substitutions noted for the other three beginner species.

**1. Enclosure: 4'×2'×2' PVC, minimum.** Glass tanks lose heat too aggressively and look smaller from the animal's side. Dubia Caviar, Zen Habitats, and Custom Cages all make appropriate PVC enclosures. *(Crested gecko: vertical 18"×18"×24" minimum. Leopard gecko: 36"×18"×18" minimum. Ball python: 4'×2'×2' adult.)*

**2. UVB: Arcadia ProT5 12% or Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 10.0**, full-length fixture, replaced annually.

[Get an Arcadia ProT5 12% kit on Amazon →](https://lizard.com/go/amazon/arcadia-pro-t5-12-uvb-kit)

**3. Basking bulb: 100W halogen flood**, not a "basking bulb." Standard halogen flood gives the IR-A wavelengths reptiles thermoregulate by; coloured "night bulbs" are a marketing fiction.

[Get a halogen flood basking bulb on Amazon →](https://lizard.com/go/amazon/halogen-flood-100w)

**4. Thermostat: Herpstat 2 or Inkbird ITC-308.** Pulse-proportional for ceramic, on/off for halogen. The basking lamp goes on a *dimming* thermostat (not on/off), which means a Herpstat — a $300 piece of kit that justifies itself the first time it prevents a basking lamp from spiking to 130 °F.

[Get an Inkbird ITC-308 thermostat on Amazon →](https://lizard.com/go/amazon/inkbird-itc-308-thermostat)

**5. Three hides minimum.** Hot end, cool end, humid hide for shedding. Cork bark and stacked stone slate look better than the plastic logs and last longer.

**6. A digital temperature gun (infrared thermometer) and a digital hygrometer with probe.** Two pieces of equipment, $40 total, that turn husbandry from guessing into knowing.

[Get an IR temp gun on Amazon →](https://lizard.com/go/amazon/etekcity-infrared-thermometer)

**7. A Solarmeter 6.5 if your budget allows.** Single most expensive item, single best return on investment.

[Get a Solarmeter 6.5 on Amazon →](https://lizard.com/go/amazon/solarmeter-6-5-uvi)

The total kit for a bearded dragon, done correctly, lands around $700–$1000 — and lasts a decade.

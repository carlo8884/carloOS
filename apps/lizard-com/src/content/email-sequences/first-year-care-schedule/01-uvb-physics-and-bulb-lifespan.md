The most common first-year husbandry failure is also the most invisible: a UVB bulb that still glows, but no longer emits useful UVB.

Here is what the published data actually says.

**The UVB band that matters.** Vitamin D3 synthesis in reptile skin requires UVB photons in the 280–315 nm band, with peak conversion efficiency around 297 nm. Visible light is 400–700 nm. A bulb that still produces visible light tells you nothing about its UVB output — the two bands degrade at very different rates.

**Published lifespans.** Solarmeter (the standard reptile UV-index meter manufacturer) has published UV-index readings across major bulb lines over time. The pattern is consistent across replicate measurements:

- **T5 HO linear fluorescent** (Arcadia, Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 HO 10.0/12.0) — useful UVB output for roughly 12 months under typical 10–12 hour photoperiods. Output decline is gradual, so the bulb still looks fine well past replacement.
- **T8 linear fluorescent** (older ReptiSun 10.0 T8, Exo Terra ReptiGlo T8) — 6–9 months. Lower starting UV index than T5 HO and steeper decline.
- **Compact fluorescent (CFL) coil bulbs** — 6 months. Some are now reformulated, but the published measurements have been consistent over multiple Solarmeter test series.
- **Mercury-vapor (MVB)** — manufacturer-specified, typically 12 months. Bulbs that fail early are common; replace at the first sign of erratic warm-up.

**The "still glowing" trap.** A T5 HO bulb at 18 months still emits visible light at near-full brightness. UV index at the basking spot, however, can have dropped from a starting UVI of 4–6 down to 1–2 — enough to keep the animal looking healthy short-term, not enough to prevent metabolic bone disease over months.

**The habit.** Replace on the published interval whether or not you own a UV meter. If you do own one (a Solarmeter 6.5 is the standard), re-measure at the 6-month mark and decide. Either way, mark the install date on the bulb with a Sharpie. The week-by-week checkbox in your schedule is there so this becomes automatic.

**What about Ferguson Zones?** Ferguson Zones (Ferguson et al., 2010) classify species by the UV index they self-select in the wild — Zone 1 (cryptic, e.g. crested gecko) through Zone 4 (high-exposure, e.g. uromastyx, beardie). The Zone determines target UVI at the basking site; the bulb lifespan above determines when that target is no longer being met regardless of fixture.

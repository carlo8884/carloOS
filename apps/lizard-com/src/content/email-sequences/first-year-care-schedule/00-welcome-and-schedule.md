You signed up for the Lizard.com Reptile First-Year Care Schedule. Welcome.

**The schedule is attached as a PDF** (`reptile-first-year-care-schedule.pdf`). Print it. Tape it inside a kitchen cabinet or above the enclosure. It covers 52 weeks across four quarters — acclimation, feeding by life stage, UVB replacement, calcium and D3 dosing, temperature and humidity logging, vet checkpoints, and equipment maintenance.

**The frame.** Most first-year reptile mortality is preventable. The husbandry literature is unusually clear on this — case-series in the *Journal of Herpetological Medicine and Surgery* and the husbandry chapters in Mader's *Reptile Medicine and Surgery* (3rd ed.) repeatedly land on the same short list of causes:

- Inconsistent UVB replacement. The bulb still glows, but its UVB output has fallen below the level needed for vitamin D3 synthesis.
- Missed or wrong calcium dosing. Either no dust, or the wrong form (Ca + D3 when UVB is adequate, or Ca-no-D3 when the animal has no UVB).
- Gradual temperature drift. A degree per month, invisible without a log.
- Undiagnosed parasite burdens. Most new reptiles arrive with something. A fecal float at the first vet visit catches it.

The schedule converts those four "invisible until disaster" items into a weekly checkbox. The schedule tells you what to do. The emails over the next ~90 days explain why.

**This week.** Don't try to do everything on the schedule at once. Read through it once. Pick the two items that aren't already in your routine. Do those.

If your enclosure was bought used, or your animal came from a chain pet store, your two items are almost certainly: (1) confirm UVB bulb type, brand, and install date, (2) schedule a fecal float at an ARAV-affiliated vet.

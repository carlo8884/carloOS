You signed up for the Ferret.com First-Year Schedule. Welcome.

**The schedule is attached as a PDF** (`ferret-first-year-schedule.pdf`). Print it. Stick it on the fridge or the cage door. It covers all 52 weeks of the first year — vaccinations, vet visits, neuter timing, dental routine onset, nail trim cadence, diet milestones, and the run-up to the long-term health monitoring window that opens around age 3.

Ferrets are unusual pets. They are obligate carnivores. They need an exotic-pet vet, not a general small-animal vet. They need vaccines, dental care, and lifelong monitoring that most pet owners do not expect. And while their median lifespan is 5–8 years (Quesenberry &amp; Carpenter, *Ferrets, Rabbits, and Rodents: Clinical Medicine and Surgery*, Saunders), the first year is when the habits that determine the next several get built.

Over the next ~90 days I will send you eight short emails. Each one covers a single topic in depth — vaccines, finding an exotic-pet vet, ferret-proofing, diet, dental care, the insulinoma watch window, and a closing email with the full Ferret.com library.

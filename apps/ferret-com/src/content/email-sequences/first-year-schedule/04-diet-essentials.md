Diet is the single largest controllable input on a ferret's lifespan. It is also the place where new owners — given mostly good intentions — accidentally do the most damage, usually by treating a ferret as a small cat and feeding cat food, or worse, by offering fruit and "ferret treats" sweetened with sugar.

**The macronutrient targets** (widely cited in exotic-pet veterinary references including Quesenberry &amp; Carpenter and the *Journal of Exotic Pet Medicine*):

- **Protein: 32–40%** dry-matter basis, primarily animal-sourced
- **Fat: 18–22%** dry-matter basis, animal-sourced (ferrets metabolize fat as their primary energy substrate)
- **Carbohydrate: under 3%** — most supermarket "ferret food" runs 15–30% carb, which is a defect, not a feature
- **Taurine:** supplemented (like cat food — ferrets do not synthesize adequate taurine endogenously)

**The "first 3 ingredients" rule.** If you remember nothing else, remember this. Read the ingredient panel on any kibble you are considering. The first 3–5 ingredients should be named animal proteins or named animal fats. If the first ingredient is a grain ("ground corn", "brewers rice", "wheat") or a plant protein concentrate ("corn gluten meal", "pea protein"), the formula is not appropriate as a sole diet — regardless of the marketing on the bag.

**The commercial diet ladder.**

- **Premium tier:** Wysong Epigen 90, Wysong Archetype, Orijen Cat &amp; Kitten (used off-label by many keepers). Animal-first, low-carb, no grain.
- **Mid tier:** Marshall Premium Ferret Diet, ZuPreem Premium Ferret Diet. Imperfect ingredient panels but in range.
- **Avoid:** Supermarket "ferret food" with corn or rice in the first three ingredients, plus any "fruit and vegetable" ferret treat.

**What NOT to feed — the hard list.**

- **Fruit of any kind.** Sugars trigger insulin release; chronic exposure stresses pancreatic beta cells over years. This is the single most important rule and the one most new owners break first.
- **Vegetables.** Ferrets do not digest plant cell walls. Carrots, peas, sweet potato — all inappropriate.
- **Grains.** Corn, rice, wheat, oats, barley.
- **Dog food.** Protein adequate, taurine inadequate. Long-term feeding produces dilated cardiomyopathy risk.
- **Most cat food.** Adult cat kibbles are typically too low in protein and too high in plant carbohydrate. High-protein kitten food is a usable stop-gap; it is not a long-term solution.
- **Dairy** (loose stool — most adult ferrets are lactose intolerant), **chocolate, xylitol, onion, garlic, caffeine.**

**The insulinoma-prevention angle.** Insulinoma is the most common neoplasm reported in middle-aged ferrets. The working hypothesis in the exotic-pet literature is that chronic dietary carbohydrate load drives sustained insulin secretion, which over years contributes to beta-cell hyperplasia and eventually insulinoma. The evidence is associational rather than experimentally proven — but the recommendation is consistent across exotic-vet sources: minimize dietary carbohydrate from kithood onward. The leverage is highest in year one because the dietary pattern is being established.

**Food imprinting.** Ferrets imprint on the foods they eat in the first ~6 months and become reluctant to switch later. The practical implication: rotate among 2–3 acceptable brands during the first year so the adult ferret does not refuse to eat if the preferred brand is reformulated or discontinued.

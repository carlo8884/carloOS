Two vaccines matter in the first year: canine distemper and rabies. Both are core. Both are non-optional in any household where the ferret will ever interact with a person other than the owner, and the reasons are different.

**Canine distemper.** Distemper is uniformly fatal in ferrets. Unlike in dogs, there is no recovery and no treatment beyond palliation. The standard kit series is three boosters between roughly 6–8 weeks and 14–16 weeks (American Ferret Association / Association of Exotic Mammal Veterinarians guidance), then an annual booster. Only USDA-licensed ferret-labelled distemper vaccines should be used (Purevax Ferret Distemper is the historical reference product; availability shifts year to year).

**Rabies.** Single dose at 12 weeks or older, then annually. The USDA-licensed product is Imrab 3. The reason rabies vaccination matters even for indoor ferrets is not the disease risk inside your home — it is the legal floor for managing bite incidents. In most US states an unvaccinated ferret that bites a person can face mandatory euthanasia or extended quarantine under public-health law, regardless of how implausible actual exposure was. The vaccine is a one-page document that prevents that outcome.

**The reaction footnote.** Ferrets have a higher rate of vaccine-associated anaphylactic reactions than dogs or cats. The Greenacre 2003 *JAAHA* paper ("Incidence of adverse events in ferrets vaccinated with distemper or rabies vaccine") is the most-cited reference here. Practical consequence: most exotic-pet vets give vaccines one at a time (never distemper and rabies in the same visit), pre-medicate with diphenhydramine, and observe the ferret in-clinic for 20–30 minutes after the injection. A reaction looks like: vomiting, hyperemia (red ears or paws), lethargy, weakness, or collapse — usually within 5–60 minutes. It is treatable in-clinic. It is not treatable in your car on the way home.

**What to do.** Use this email as a checklist when you talk to your exotic-pet vet:

- Confirm the vaccines are ferret-labelled USDA-licensed products
- Confirm distemper and rabies are given on separate visits (typical interval: 2–3 weeks apart)
- Ask whether the clinic pre-medicates with diphenhydramine — most do
- Confirm 20–30 minute post-vaccine observation in the clinic
- Get the rabies certificate filed somewhere you can find it (this is the document that matters in a bite-incident workflow)

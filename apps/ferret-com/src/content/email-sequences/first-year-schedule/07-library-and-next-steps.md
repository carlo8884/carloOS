Final email in the series.

If you have been following along, your ferret has now been with you for roughly three months and the first-year foundation — exotic-pet vet relationship, vaccine schedule, dental routine, low-carb diet pattern, ferret-proofed environment — is either in place or you know what is missing.

Most of what comes next in the first year is **keeping the routine and going deeper as questions come up**. The library is below. Bookmark what looks relevant.

**Care fundamentals**

- [Ferret Diet Basics](https://ferret.com/care/diet-basics) — macronutrient targets, brand ladder, what NOT to feed
- [Ferret Cage Setup](https://ferret.com/care/cage-setup) — multi-level housing, ferret-proofing the surrounding room
- [Insulinoma in Ferrets](https://ferret.com/health/insulinoma) — the long-term watch condition

**Year-two reading (queue these up)**

- Adrenal disease in ferrets — incidence in US ferret populations, deslorelin (Suprelorin) implant protocol, surgical vs. medical management
- Adrenal vs. insulinoma differential — the symptom-overlap problem
- ECE (epizootic catarrhal enteritis) and other ferret-specific GI conditions
- Anesthesia risk and how to choose a clinic for ferret dentals or surgeries

**Equipment timelines**

The schedule emails you have already received covered diet and dental tools. As your ferret moves into adolescence and adulthood, two equipment categories tend to come up:

- **Cage upgrades.** Many keepers start with a single-level kit-friendly cage and move to a Ferret Nation or Critter Nation double-unit by month 6–9. We maintain reviews of the major options at [Ferret.com](https://ferret.com/) — see our reviews section as it rolls out. (Where we link to a vendor, we may earn affiliate commission on qualifying purchases at no cost to you; disclosed in our affiliate policy.)
- **Diet transitions.** As you rotate among the 2–3 acceptable kibbles from email 04, having one bag of premium-tier kibble and one bag of mid-tier kibble open at once is the rotation pattern most keepers settle into.

**Community.** The ferret-keeping community lives largely in three places: the American Ferret Association (AFA) site and forums, regional ferret rescue networks, and a handful of long-running ferret keeper subreddits and Facebook groups. As Ferret.com grows we will be adding directory pages to make these easier to find — and our sister site Ferrets.com (community + rescue directory) will host the community side once it ships.

**One last note.** You will come across confident-sounding online advice that contradicts almost everything in this course. "Ferrets do fine on cat food." "Indoor ferrets don't need rabies vaccines." "You can use human toothpaste in a pinch." None of those are correct in the contemporary exotic-pet veterinary literature. When in doubt, the source is the standard — Quesenberry &amp; Carpenter, the *Journal of Exotic Pet Medicine*, *Veterinary Clinics of North America: Exotic Animal Practice*, the AFA, and the AEMV.

The schedule tells you to socialize your puppy between weeks 8 and 16. What it doesn't tell you is the most common way owners get this wrong.

It's not under-exposure. It's **flooding**.

Flooding is what happens when a well-meaning owner takes the puppy to a busy farmer's market, holds them while strangers crowd around, and tells themselves "great, ten new people in one go." The puppy didn't get to choose. The puppy couldn't retreat. The puppy filed "strangers approaching" as a thing that happens without consent.

A flooded puppy looks calm in the moment — frozen, actually — and then turns reactive at 7 or 8 months when adolescence rewires the threat system.

The fix is one rule: **the puppy chooses the approach.**

You walk to the edge of the new thing. You sit down. You let the puppy decide whether to come investigate. If they back away, you let them back away — and you stay calm, because your calm is the data they're using.

Ten exposures a week, puppy-led, beats fifty exposures a week, owner-led, every single time. This is the consensus position from the AVSAB (American Veterinary Society of Animal Behavior) puppy socialization statement.

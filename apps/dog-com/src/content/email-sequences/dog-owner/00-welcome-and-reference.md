You signed up on Dog.com. Welcome.

This is a working dog-owner reference site — breed profiles, condition references, training and behaviour guidance, food evaluations, insurance comparisons, product reviews. Drawn from peer-reviewed veterinary literature, AAHA / AVMA preventive-medicine standards, AAFCO labelling rules, and primary-source breed reference material. Not a marketplace; not a clinical advice service.

For anything specific to your dog, defer to a licensed veterinarian. The site is reference; clinical decisions are your vet's.

Over the next three weeks you will get four more short emails — each on one decision that comes up for almost every dog owner: food, the annual vet visit, pet insurance, and recognising pain.

---

**What lives on Dog.com today**

- **Breeds.** Practical breed pages — temperament, work history, common health issues, what each breed asks of its household.
- **Conditions.** A hub of common medical conditions, early-warning signs, when to call out a vet, the typical workup.
- **Food.** Ingredient-panel evaluations, AAFCO statement reading, life-stage and condition-specific feeding.
- **Insurance.** Carrier comparisons, what the policies actually cover, the math on when insurance pays for itself.
- **Training and behaviour.** Force-free training references, behaviour problem assessment, when to call a credentialed behaviourist (CAAB / Dip ACVB).
- **Reviews.** Documented-spec product comparisons across food, supplies, supplements, gear. Methodology disclosed on every review.

---

**What this email sequence will not do**

- Recommend a specific dog food for your dog (we don't know its history, breed, conditions, age).
- Substitute for a licensed veterinarian.
- Push you toward a specific insurer or affiliate brand.

What it will do: anchor the four highest-volume dog-owner decisions to public sources and to the Dog.com reference pages that go deeper.

— Dog.com Editorial

Why does the puppy world keep saying "weeks 8 to 16"? Because the dog's brain is uniquely receptive during that period in a way it never is again.

The short version: during weeks 8–16, the puppy's brain treats *novelty* and *safety* as the default association — anything new is filed as "this is normal life". After ~16 weeks the default flips: novelty starts getting filed as "this could be a threat, default to caution". A dog that meets 100 different people, surfaces, sounds, and other dogs before week 16 grows up calm. A dog that meets 10 of each grows up reactive — not because of any single bad experience, but because everything after week 16 is novel.

This is why under-socialization during this window is repeatedly cited among the leading drivers of adult behavioural problems and shelter surrenders (ASPCA, HSUS shelter intake studies).

**Two things you can do this week:**

1. **The "ten new things" rule.** Every week between weeks 9 and 16, expose the puppy to ten things they haven't seen before. People in hats. Umbrellas opening. Wheelchairs. Lawn mowers. Bicycles. Children running. A vacuum on. Walking on tile, grass, gravel, sand, metal grates, wet leaves. Each exposure: short, positive, optional retreat.
2. **Puppy class.** Not for "obedience" — for the structured exposure to other vaccinated puppies and humans. AAHA position statement explicitly endorses early puppy classes starting at 7–8 weeks after the first vaccination, ahead of complete vaccination, because the behavioural risk of under-socialization is greater than the disease risk in a clean class environment.

**What to avoid.** Forcing interactions. If the puppy backs away from something, let them back away — then approach the thing yourself from 6 feet away and let the puppy come investigate when they decide. The puppy choosing to approach > you placing the puppy.

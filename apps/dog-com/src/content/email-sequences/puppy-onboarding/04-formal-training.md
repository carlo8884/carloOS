By now your puppy is around 11–13 weeks old and the foundation is in place: name recognition, crate familiarity, sleep schedule, first round of vaccinations. This week: formal training starts.

**Rule 1: short sessions, multiple times per day.** Three to five minutes, three to five times per day. A puppy's attention span at 12 weeks is ~5 minutes. Anything longer and you're teaching them to disengage. Tying sessions to mealtime works well — train just before feeding, use the puppy's kibble as the reward currency.

**Rule 2: positive reinforcement only.** Reward what you want, ignore or redirect what you don't. The AVSAB position statement on the use of punishment in training is unambiguous: reward-based training is more effective, has fewer side effects, and is associated with lower stress markers and fewer fear-based behavioural problems. Choke chains, prong collars, e-collars, and "alpha" / "dominance" methods are obsolete and harmful.

**Rule 3: order matters.** Teach in this sequence:

1. **Name** — already started (week 8).
2. **Sit** — kibble above the nose, slowly back. As the head goes up, the rear goes down. Mark, reward, release.
3. **Hand target** — present a flat palm. Puppy touches it with nose. Mark, reward. This becomes recall in disguise.
4. **Down** — from sit, lure to the floor between front paws.
5. **Stay (1–3 seconds)** — sit, brief pause, mark, reward. Build duration before distance, build distance before distraction.
6. **Recall (come)** — only after hand target is solid. Always reward, never call to punish.
7. **Leave it** — the most under-taught and most useful command.

The point is not the trick — it's the training relationship. A puppy who has done five 3-minute sessions a day for two months is a puppy who pays attention to you. That's the actual deliverable.

**Pick a method (not a person).** "Positive reinforcement" is a body of method, not a brand. If a trainer mentions dominance, pack leadership, prong collars, or e-collars, leave. CPDT-KA, KPA-CTP, and CCPDT credentials are reliable signals.

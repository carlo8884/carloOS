If your puppy is now around 12–14 weeks: it's almost time to drop one meal. Here is the schedule.

**Standard schedule (most breeds).** Three meals/day until ~16 weeks. Two meals/day from 16 weeks through adult life. Never one — single-meal-per-day adults are at significantly elevated bloat (GDV) risk in deep-chested breeds, and the long fasting window is associated with bilious vomiting in others.

**Drop the middle meal.** When you move from three meals to two, drop the midday meal — keep breakfast and dinner. Maintain consistent feeding times.

**Large-breed exception.** If you have a large or giant breed puppy (>50 lb expected adult weight: Labradors, Goldens, Shepherds, Danes, Mastiffs, Saints, Newfoundlands, etc.), you should be on a **large-breed-formulated puppy food** for the entire growth period — typically until 12–18 months depending on breed. Adult food too early (or regular puppy food without controlled calcium/phosphorus) causes accelerated growth and is implicated in the development of hip dysplasia, OCD, and panosteitis.

WSAVA's nutritional assessment guidelines are the reference for selecting a brand. The brands that consistently meet WSAVA's criteria for veterinary nutritionist involvement, feeding trials, and published research are Royal Canin, Purina Pro Plan, Hill's Science Diet, Iams, and Eukanuba. Many "premium" boutique brands do not meet these criteria.

**Watch the body condition score, not the bowl.** A puppy at the correct weight has a slight waist when viewed from above, ribs you can feel under a thin layer of fat but not see at rest, and visible "tuck" up to the belly. Overfeeding at 3 months sets the metabolic baseline for life — POMC-mutation Labradors in particular are nearly impossible to slim down once overfed early.

**What about raw / freeze-dried / fresh-cooked / grain-free?**
- **Raw** has documented bacterial contamination risk (Salmonella, Listeria, E. coli) and is not recommended by AAHA or AVMA for households with immunocompromised members.
- **Grain-free** is associated with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) in some dogs per the FDA's ongoing investigation. Avoid grain-free diets unless prescribed for a diagnosed grain allergy (which is rare — most "allergies" are environmental or beef/chicken protein).
- **Fresh-cooked subscriptions** (The Farmer's Dog, Ollie, etc.) are convenient. Several brands have veterinary nutritionists on staff and meet WSAVA criteria; check each.

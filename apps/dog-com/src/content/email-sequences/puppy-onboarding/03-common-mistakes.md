Five common things that look harmless at 10 weeks and create real problems at 10 months.

**1. Skipping the crate because the puppy cries.** The crate teaches "this is where I am calm and alone". A puppy that never learns to be alone becomes a dog with separation anxiety. Use the crate for every nap, starting on day one. Crying is normal for the first 3–5 days. It stops.

**2. Picking the puppy up to interrupt anything you don't like.** Biting the carpet, biting your hand, jumping. If you scoop the puppy up every time, you train the puppy that the response to overstimulation is "be carried". You also miss the chance to teach the actual skill — disengaging on their own. Better: redirect to a chew, walk away briefly, or crate for a nap if the puppy is overtired (90% of "biting problems" at 10 weeks are tired puppies).

**3. Free-feeding (leaving food in the bowl all day).** Two problems: (a) house training becomes guesswork — without scheduled meals you can't predict elimination windows; (b) the food loses value as a training reward. Three scheduled meals (eventually two), measured to the bag's puppy-weight chart, and pick up the bowl after 10 minutes if uneaten.

**4. Letting the puppy meet every dog at the dog park.** Random dogs at the dog park is the single highest-risk socialization activity. One bad encounter at 12 weeks can install dog reactivity for life. Trade the dog park for a puppy class (curated peers, vaccinated, supervised) until at least 6 months.

**5. Punishing the warning growl.** A growling puppy is communicating discomfort. Punish the growl and you don't fix the discomfort — you remove the warning system. Then one day the dog bites with no warning. Always respect a growl: get distance, give space, work on the underlying trigger separately. Resource guarding, fear of handling, and stranger discomfort all show up first as a growl.

— Dog.com Editorial

A saddle is an heirloom or it is an expense. Whether yours becomes the first or the second depends almost entirely on care.

The kit below is the one we keep in the tack room. It is not flashy. It works.

**1. A glycerin saddle soap.** Belvoir Tack Cleaner bar, kept on a damp sponge. Used after every ride to remove sweat and salt before they oxidize the leather.

[Get Belvoir Tack Cleaner at Dover →](https://saddle.com/go/dover/belvoir-tack-cleaner-bar)

**2. A neatsfoot-free conditioner.** Effax Leder-Balsam or Higher Standards Leather Care. Neatsfoot oil over-darkens, over-softens, and rots stitching over a decade — we don't recommend it on a quality saddle.

[Get Effax Leder-Balsam at SmartPak →](https://saddle.com/go/smartpak/effax-leder-balsam)

**3. A saddle stand for storage.** Not a rack on the wall — a proper full-shape stand that supports the panels and tree. A saddle stored on a narrow rack distorts at the cantle within five years.

[Get a full-shape saddle stand at Dover →](https://saddle.com/go/dover/wooden-saddle-stand)

**4. A breathable cover, not plastic.** Plastic traps humidity and grows mildew. Heavy cotton sheeting is the standard.

[Get a cotton saddle cover at SmartPak →](https://saddle.com/go/smartpak/cotton-saddle-cover)

**5. A pad system matched to the saddle, not the horse.** Half-pads should *correct* fit only as a short-term measure between professional flocking visits. They are not a permanent solution. ThinLine and Mattes are the two pad makers whose construction we trust for that interim role.

[Get a ThinLine half-pad at SmartPak →](https://saddle.com/go/smartpak/thinline-trifecta-half-pad)

**6. An annual fitter visit.** Not a product, but the most important line item in the budget. A horse's back changes with age, fitness, season, and work. A saddle fit on day one is rarely the saddle fit at month eighteen.

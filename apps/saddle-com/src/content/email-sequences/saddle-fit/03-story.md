A reader, Eleanor, shared the eighteen-month story of fitting her warmblood mare. Edited for length, with her permission:

**Saddle 1.** Off the rack, medium tree. The vendor said it would "fit anything." The mare started flinching at girth-up within two weeks.

**Saddle 2.** A flocking adjustment on the first one. Better for a month. Then the same flinch returned. The flocking had compressed.

**Saddle 3.** A different brand, wide tree. Visibly too wide at the points — sat low at the withers. Returned in a fortnight.

**Saddle 4.** A consignment piece, an older English brand. Looked beautiful. Bridged badly on the dynamic test. A professional fitter caught it in five minutes.

**Saddle 5.** A custom-tree synthetic. Comfortable for the mare, useful as a schooling saddle, but Eleanor never quite loved riding in it.

**Saddle 6.** A demo from a master saddler. Two visits, two adjustments. The mare softened through the topline within three rides. They put a deposit on it. The brand discontinued the model six weeks later.

**Saddle 7.** A near-equivalent from the same maker. The fitter measured both horse and rider, built a tree spec, and waited eleven weeks for delivery. It arrived. It fit. Eleanor cried.

**Saddle 8.** A second of the same model, eight months later, for a second horse that had come into her care. It needed three flocking visits to settle in. It now fits.

What Eleanor said at the end of the writeup: "The saddle isn't furniture. It's the conversation between three living things — horse, rider, and the saddler who made it. Treat it that way and it lasts forty years."

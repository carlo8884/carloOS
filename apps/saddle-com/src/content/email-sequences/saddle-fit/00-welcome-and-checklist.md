**Before your next try-on appointment, prep these four things.** Almost every fitting that goes sideways goes sideways because one of these was skipped.

1. **Clean horse.** A muddy or freshly clipped horse can mask sweat patterns and palpation findings. Groom before the fitter arrives. No fly spray on the saddle area on appointment morning — it changes the friction at the panel-coat interface and the sweat read.

2. **Your tack, every piece.** Bring the girth you ride in, the pad you ride in, the breastplate or crupper if you use one, and the bridle. Fit changes with the actual setup. A perfect static fit with a fresh foam pad can fail with the half-pad you actually own.

3. **Your riding clothes.** Boots, breeches, helmet. The fitter needs to see your seat in your actual riding position, not your jeans-and-sneakers position. Two seconds of standing in the stirrups in real footwear tells the fitter what your daily ride looks like.

4. **A video assistant.** One person with a phone, recording side-on at the walk and trot, and behind from across the arena at the trot. The fitter sees what's in front of them; the video catches what only shows in motion. Save the file — it is your before-and-after.

---

**The order to read the checklist.** Points 1–7 are the static check (saddle on, girthed, no rider). Point 4 (channel width) is repeated with the rider mounted. Points 8 and 12 happen after light work. Points 9–11 are interleaved through the ride.

You're not learning the checklist to replace a fitter. You're learning it so you can be a useful participant in the fitting and an alert observer between fittings.

— Saddle.com Editorial

There is no such thing as a brand that "fits all horses". There is, however, such a thing as a brand whose typical tree shape is a sensible starting point for a given horse build.

Brand-fit personality is a *short list* discipline. It narrows what saddles to ask the fitter to bring, not what saddle to buy.

---

**The honest brand-fit map.** All starting points only — your fitter is the source of truth for your horse.

- **Stubben** — German construction, traditional flat-rocker trees, well-suited to *narrow to medium* withers and *high-withered* horses (Stubben heritage is Thoroughbred and warmblood). Models like the Genesis, Tristan, and Roxane are workhorses; their adjustable-tree CS / Junior line offers a tree-width range without the cost of a custom. Strong used market in the $1,500–$3,500 band.

- **Pessoa** — French-Brazilian heritage, more rocker than Stubben, typically *medium*-tree fit. Long-loved for jumpers and event horses with moderate builds. Pessoa A/O and Tomboy are the broadly-fitting models; the Gen-X and Mademoiselle Alixe have changeable gullets. Strong resale.

- **Wintec / Bates** — Synthetic / part-synthetic with the *Easy-Change Gullet System*. The single most flexible option for a growing horse, a horse changing topline, or an owner who genuinely needs a saddle that can be re-set as the horse changes. Wintec sits at the budget-conscious end; Bates is the leather-equipped sibling at a higher price.

- **County** — North American, known for *high-withered* and asymmetric-shoulder fit, with strong customisability through their dealer network. Particularly common in dressage. Higher price band; longer waitlist on custom builds.

- **Custom Saddlery** — As the name says: bespoke from the start. The starting point for *round-backed*, *mutton-withered*, or otherwise hard-to-fit horses where a standard tree doesn't sit right. Higher price, longer lead time, but the right tool when the horse is unusual.

- **Albion, Schleese, Black Country** — The other premium-and-customisable cluster. Worth knowing exist, worth asking the fitter about when your horse is in the awkward middle of the standard tree-width distribution.

---

**What this list is for.** When a fitter visits, you can usefully say: "He's a high-withered TB cross with a slightly hollow back behind the shoulder. I'd like to try a Stubben Genesis, the County Conquest, and one Schleese option if you have one." That conversation moves the fitting forward by an hour.

**What this list is *not* for.** Buying. No one in this list is a "buy without trying" recommendation. Saddle-fit research is unambiguous: brand reputation does not predict whether a *specific* saddle fits a *specific* horse. Brand-fit personality is a starting filter, not a verdict.

**Where the affiliate-aware retailers fit.**

- **SmartPak Equine** — strong on new English saddles, demo programs, return policies. Their saddle-fit consultation service is brand-employed (treat as informed advice, not independent assessment) but the demo / return logistics are excellent.
- **Dover Saddlery** — broadest English inventory, large used / consignment side, in-store fitter availability at some locations.
- **Riding Warehouse** — strong on accessories and the less-premium English range; good for first saddles in the Wintec / Bates / Collegiate band.
- **Stubben North America, Pessoa-owned outlets, County dealer network** — the brand-direct channels. Cheaper than aftermarket in some cases, more expensive in others. Always negotiable.

**This week.** Before your next fitting, write down your horse's build in three lines: wither (narrow / medium / broad / unusually high), back (flat / curved / slightly hollow), shoulder (laid-back / steep / asymmetric). Bring that to the fitter along with the brand short-list. You'll get more candidates to try and better data from each one.

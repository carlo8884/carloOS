Final email in the saddle-fit series.

If you've been with us the whole way: you have a printable 12-point checklist on your wall, you've read sweat patterns at least once, you know the line between self-check and a credentialed fitter, you've located the two nearest SMS or MSA fitters to you, and you know roughly where your saddle sits in the re-flock-vs-replace decision.

That is the working knowledge most riders never accumulate.

---

**The 12 points, one more time.**

1. Wither clearance — 2–3 fingers, full length
2. Panel contact, left — even, no bridging, no rocking
3. Panel contact, right — symmetric to left
4. Channel width — at least 3 fingers, daylight visible under load
5. Tree angle at the points — follows the scapula
6. Balance point — level or pommel-low, cantle slightly above pommel
7. Billet alignment — perpendicular at the natural girth groove
8. Sweat pattern after light work — even, no dry patches
9. Stirrup hang — vertical under a balanced rider
10. Gullet plate angle (adjustable systems) — confirmed by fitter
11. Rocker / curve match — panel curve matches back curve
12. Dynamic-fit + post-ride hair check — ruffled / broken / white hair = pressure history

Print the PDF again if you've lost it. Bring it to every fitting from now on.

**The one thing to take away.**

The horse cannot tell you the saddle hurts. It can pin its ears, dip its back, refuse the canter, bite the cross-tie when girthed, or just become *quietly sour* over the months. Every one of those signals is a sentence the horse is writing about its tack — and the first responsibility of the rider is to check the tack before the training. The checklist is your shortcut to that check.

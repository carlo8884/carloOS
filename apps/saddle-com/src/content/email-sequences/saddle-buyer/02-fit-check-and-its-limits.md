There are four things a horse owner can check before calling a fitter. They are not a replacement for a qualified Society of Master Saddlers fitter; they are an early-warning system. If any of these is failing, the saddle does not fit, and no pad will rescue it.

---

**1. Wither clearance.** Place the saddle on the horse without a pad, girthed to working tension. Look down from above the pommel. You should see 2–3 fingers of clearance between the pommel arch and the highest point of the wither, maintained the full length of the wither. Too narrow (pommel close to or on the wither): the tree is squeezing. Too wide (4+ fingers, pommel dropping low): the tree is too wide and will rock.

**2. Panel contact.** Slide a flat hand between the panel and the horse's back, front-to-rear, both sides. You want consistent even resistance — not too easy, not too hard, and most importantly even from end to end. The most common defect is bridging: easy under the middle of the panel, firm at the front and rear. The saddle is acting like a bridge, concentrating pressure at two points.

**3. Spinal channel clearance.** Put a rider in the saddle. Look from behind through the gullet between the panels. You should see daylight the full length, at least three fingers wide, even with the rider's weight. Panels closing toward the midline under load is direct pressure on the spinous processes — a serious problem that gets worse, not better.

**4. Balance.** From the side, with rider mounted, the deepest point of the seat should be roughly level or very slightly lower than the pommel. The cantle should be a touch higher than the pommel — never lower. Tipped forward (cantle high): rider behind the motion, seat bones into the back. Tipped back (cantle low): rider on the fork, wither pressure.

---

**What the 4-point check can't tell you**

- Whether the panel flocking is symmetrical inside the saddle (one side wool, one side compressed — common in older saddles).
- Whether the tree is broken (visible cracks are obvious; subtle tree distortion in a fall is not).
- Whether the panel material has degraded inside (compressed flocking, lumps, asymmetric breakdown).
- Whether a saddle that fits statically also fits dynamically at the trot for that horse.
- What needs to be done about a failed point — re-flock, replace, return.

Owner-level checks are an early-warning system. Failed check → call a fitter. Passed check → keep checking every 6–12 months and after every major change in the horse's condition. Horses change shape with fitness, age, season, and pregnancy; a saddle that fit 18 months ago may not now.

The accepted standard across SMS and MSA owner literature is a professional fit assessment annually, plus on any new saddle before first use.

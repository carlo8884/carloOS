A well-bought used saddle is the best value in the equestrian market. Quality European leather appreciates with use; a 10-year-old well-cared-for Stubben or Albion rides better than a new mid-tier saddle and costs a third less. A badly-bought used saddle is the most expensive purchase a rider makes — there is no warranty, there is rarely a return window, and the trade-in value of a broken tree is zero.

The difference is the inspection.

---

**The audit, in order**

**1. Tree integrity.** Set the saddle pommel-down on a soft surface. Push down on the cantle. The tree should resist firmly — if it gives or you feel any crack, the tree is broken. A broken tree is unrepairable; the saddle is firewood. Listen for any clicking, popping, or wood-on-wood sound during the push test.

**2. Panel symmetry.** Lay the saddle upside down on a flat surface. Compare the two panels visually — symmetrical curvature, similar flocking depth, no obvious low spots. Press each panel with a flat palm. Even resistance both sides; lumps, hard spots, or sudden give signal flocking damage.

**3. Stitching and billet integrity.** Inspect every stitch line for separation, particularly the billet attachment points (where the girth attaches). Billets themselves should be supple, not stiff or cracked; replacement billets are a $40–80 fix and reasonable on an otherwise sound saddle.

**4. Leather condition.** Run a hand over the seat, flaps, and underside. Leather should be supple, not stiff or sticky. Dry cracked leather is reconditionable; rot (often signaled by a mildewed smell) is not.

**5. The 4-point fit check on your horse.** A saddle that passes every other inspection point but doesn't fit your horse is the wrong saddle. This is non-negotiable — see the previous email in this sequence.

---

**Where to buy used**

- **Reputable consignment shops** specialising in used English/Western tack. They inspect on intake and stand behind the saddle.
- **Saddle fitters** — many qualified SMS or MSA fitters take used saddles on consignment from clients who upgraded. The fitter has already inspected; you get a fit assessment with the purchase.
- **Direct from owner, with a professional pre-purchase inspection.** Pay a qualified fitter $100–200 to inspect a private sale before money changes hands. This is the cheapest insurance in equestrian buying.

**Where not to buy used:** general online auction sites (eBay, Facebook Marketplace) without an inspection. No return window, no warranty, no recourse if the tree is broken.

---

**The number that matters**

A quality used saddle from a reputable source is typically priced at 50–70% of new. Quality brands (Stubben, Albion, Passier, Bates) hold value well; midtier and unknown brands depreciate faster. If the asking price is more than 70% of comparable new, walk away — even if the saddle is gorgeous.

— Saddle.com Editorial

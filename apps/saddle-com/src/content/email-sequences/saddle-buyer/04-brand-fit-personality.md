The brand layer of saddle fit is real. Two saddles with identical specifications on paper feel materially different to ride — seat-bone width, the twist, the leather, the stitch character. Most riders pick a brand they're loyal to because the brand fit their seat and their horse's back the first time, and the loyalty makes sense in that case.

But brand-fit personality is the **third** decision (discipline → tree shape → brand). Use this cheat sheet as a starting list of brands to try given your discipline and horse build, then let the saddle-fit appointments narrow it from there.

---

**A note on adjustable-tree saddles**

Wintec and Bates Caprilli systems use a changeable gullet that lets a single saddle accommodate several tree widths. The benefit is real for a growing young horse or a string of differently-built lesson horses; the trade-off is a slightly less precise fit than a properly-flocked custom-tree saddle in a single horse. Excellent value at the entry-to-mid price point.

**A note on custom-made saddles**

A fully custom saddle from a Society of Master Saddlers shop is the gold standard — measured to the specific horse, made over months, often a 25-year asset with maintenance. The price tag is steep ($4,500–8,000 for a high-end custom dressage saddle); the value calculation makes sense for a single horse you plan to ride for many years, not for a casual rider who may switch horses every couple of years.

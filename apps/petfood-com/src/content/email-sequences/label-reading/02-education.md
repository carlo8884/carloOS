The single most useful framework for evaluating a pet food brand isn't on the label. It's the **WSAVA Global Nutrition Committee's manufacturer questionnaire** — five questions that a brand can either answer or not.

The five questions, paraphrased:

1. **Do you employ a full-time qualified nutritionist?** Specifically: a PhD in animal nutrition, or a board-certified veterinary nutritionist (ACVIM Nutrition / ECVCN).
2. **Who formulates your diets, and what are their credentials?** A nutritionist's *involvement* is not the same as a nutritionist *formulating* the diet.
3. **Are your diets tested using AAFCO feeding trials?** Or is nutritional adequacy established by formulation only? The feeding trial is more expensive, more time-consuming, and a stronger evidence standard.
4. **Where are your foods made and what quality-control measures do you use?** Co-packing versus owned manufacturing matters; HACCP versus ISO matters.
5. **What kind of product research has been conducted, and is it published in peer-reviewed journals?** Marketing claims are not research.

The brands that publicly answer all five, consistently and in detail, are a short list: **Royal Canin, Purina Pro Plan, Hill's Science Diet, Iams, and Eukanuba.** That is not a popularity ranking — it is the answer to whether a manufacturer's nutritional standards are transparent enough to assess.

Many of the boutique brands marketed as "premium" or "natural" do not employ a qualified nutritionist, do not use feeding trials, and have not published peer-reviewed research. That is not, on its own, a reason to avoid them — but it is a reason to know what you're paying for.

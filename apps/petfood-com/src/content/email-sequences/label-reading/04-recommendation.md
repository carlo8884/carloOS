Pet food cost is usually compared by bag price. That's the wrong unit. The right unit is **cost per day** — bag price divided by (bag weight × kcal/kg of the food ÷ daily kcal need of the pet).

A worked example. A 30 lb adult dog needs roughly 800 kcal/day. A 30 lb bag of a WSAVA-compliant kibble at 3,800 kcal/kg lasts roughly 65 days. At $85 the cost is $1.31/day. A $90 boutique kibble at 3,200 kcal/kg (lower energy density) lasts only 55 days for the same dog — cost is $1.64/day. The "premium" food is 25% more expensive on the day, and the only label difference is the marketing.

This isn't an argument for cheap food. It's an argument for accurate comparison. A more expensive food that meets WSAVA's transparency standards can be worth it. The comparison should be eyes-open.

**Two practical items that actually earn their place:**

**1. An airtight food storage container.** Open-bag kibble oxidizes — the fats go rancid within 4-6 weeks, degrading the fat-soluble vitamins and palatability. An airtight bin (with a gasket, not just a lid) extends usable life dramatically. The Vittles Vault and the Iris USA airtight series are the two we've tested against open-bag controls; both held the food fresh past a 60-day window.

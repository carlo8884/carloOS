A reader named Daniel wrote in after switching his two dogs off a boutique grain-free brand. With his permission, the condensed version:

For three years Daniel fed an expensive, single-protein, grain-free kibble. The marketing was beautiful. The bag was beautiful. The dogs ate it without complaint.

The first sign anything was off was the older dog's lethargy at age seven — gradual enough that Daniel attributed it to age. The second sign was a heart murmur picked up at a wellness visit. The vet asked the question that turned out to be the relevant one: *what are you feeding?*

The brand he'd been buying was on the FDA's ongoing dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) investigation list — the open investigation into a correlation between grain-free and pulse-ingredient-heavy diets and an unusual pattern of DCM in dogs not genetically predisposed. The correlation isn't proof of causation, but it's well-documented enough that the FDA published the data set publicly in 2019 and the list has been updated since.

Daniel switched both dogs to a WSAVA-compliant kibble. The older dog's cardiac follow-up at six months showed measurable improvement in left ventricular function (per his cardiologist's echo). The vet said he could not promise the food was the cause of the improvement. He could only say: the food had been the strongest available variable to change, and the improvement followed.

What Daniel said in the writeup: "I had been buying marketing. I thought I was buying nutrition. The label didn't lie — but it didn't tell me what I needed to know."

The most common question new owners ask in week 1: *"What saddle should I buy?"*

The answer is: not yet.

Here is why. Your horse will change shape over the first 90 days you own them. They will lose or gain topline depending on previous fitness. They will muscle up in places that change wither shape, scapula position, and rib spring. A saddle bought in week 1 is being fitted to a horse that won't exist in week 12.

The right week to fit a saddle is week 10–12, after the horse has settled into your routine and the work pattern that will define the next year of their life. Until then, you ride in what the previous owner used (if it fits acceptably), or in a borrowed schooling saddle from the yard, or in nothing yet.

**Where to spend the money in weeks 1–8 instead, in priority order:**

1. **A pre-purchase vet exam and a 30-day re-check.** If you skipped the vet exam at purchase, do not skip the 30-day re-check.
2. **A farrier consultation with x-rays of all four feet.** Establish a baseline. You will refer back to those films for the next decade.
3. **A dental exam.** Teeth drive bit acceptance, head carriage, weight, and behaviour. Do this in the first 60 days.
4. **A baseline blood panel.** A single CBC + chemistry now gives you a comparison point if anything goes sideways at month six.
5. **Insurance — major medical, surgical, and mortality.** Most policies exclude pre-existing conditions diagnosed before enrollment. Sign up before the vet visits, not after.

A reader named Anna sent us her first-90-days journal after she bought her quarter horse gelding, Buster, at 38. With her permission, the condensed version:

**Week 1.** Anna didn't ride. She mucked, fed, groomed, hand-walked. She sat on the fence and watched him for an hour every evening. She read the roadmap twice. Nothing else.

**Week 2.** First yard groom of Buster was a disaster — he bit. By Friday he was nudging her pocket for treats. The grooming routine was the trust-building, not the riding.

**Week 3.** First ride. Twenty minutes at the walk. She said it was the most boring twenty minutes of her life. It was also the most important.

**Week 5.** Farrier baseline visit. Buster had been shod incorrectly for months. The new farrier corrected the angle over three cycles. Anna's vet had warned her not to expect a transformation overnight, and there wasn't one — there was a quiet, steady soundness improvement over the following twelve weeks.

**Week 6.** Dentist. Mild hooks on the upper sixes. A 40-minute float. He stopped tossing his head at the bit within two rides.

**Week 8.** First trot work. Anna fell off at the canter in week 9. She got back on and kept her stirrups for week 10.

**Week 12.** Saddle fitter. Bought a used dressage saddle on the fitter's recommendation, with the fitter's flocking adjustment built into the price. It still fits him today, three years later.

What Anna said at the end of the journal: "I did 20% of what the loudest people on the internet told me to do, and 100% of what the roadmap said. That ratio was the entire game."

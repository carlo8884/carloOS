Poor saddle fit is the single most overlooked cause of behavioural problems in ridden horses — reluctance to go forward, refusing transitions, bucking at canter, stiffness on one rein, cold-back behaviour at mounting, ears pinned at saddling. Before addressing those problems as training or behaviour, rule out pain from saddle fit.

The peer-reviewed pressure-mapping literature (Greve & Dyson, *The Veterinary Journal*, 2013; Belock et al., *The Veterinary Journal*, 2012; von Peinen et al., *Equine Veterinary Journal Supplement*, 2010) has repeatedly shown direct relationships between asymmetric or excessive saddle-pressure distribution and measurable lameness, back muscle atrophy, and gait change.

This email is the high-level framework. The full 12-point reference and the cited research sit on the [Saddle Fit Basics page](https://horses.com/guides/saddle-fit-basics).

---

**The 12-point framework — high level**

1. **Wither clearance.** 2–3 fingers above the highest point of the wither, maintained the full length.
2. **Panel contact.** Even resistance from front to rear on both sides — no bridging.
3. **Spinal channel clearance.** Daylight through the gullet under rider weight.
4. **Balance.** Seat level or slightly lower than pommel; cantle slightly higher than pommel.
5. **Tree angle vs shoulder.** Tree-point angle matches the shoulder slope; does not dig into the trapezius.
6. **Billet alignment.** Billets hang perpendicular to the ground; girth sits in the natural girth groove.
7. **Saddle position behind the shoulder.** Saddle rests 2–3 inches behind the shoulder blade — not on it.
8. **Length on the back.** Last rib is the anatomical end of the saddle bearing surface; panels should not extend past it.
9. **Symmetry under rider.** From behind, the saddle sits centred under the rider; no list to one side.
10. **Sweat-pattern read.** Even sweat after work indicates even pressure; dry patches indicate pressure points.
11. **Static-to-dynamic consistency.** A saddle that fits standing must also fit at the trot — many bridge in motion.
12. **Owner-recognisable signs of pain.** Pinned ears at saddling, cold back at mounting, refusal of canter, asymmetric muscle development.

Owner-level checks are an early-warning system. Failed check → call a fitter. Passed check → recheck every 6–12 months and after any change in the horse's condition.

---

**When to call a Society of Master Saddlers (SMS) Registered Qualified Saddle Fitter or a Master Saddlers Association (MSA) Certified Saddle Fitter**

- Any time you buy a new saddle — before it is used.
- Annually, or after significant change in the horse's condition, fitness, or topline.
- Sweat-pattern reading shows new dry patches.
- New behavioural problems under saddle that the saddle was not recently assessed against.
- Horse returning from injury or long-term rest.
- Young horse growing rapidly (topline changes significantly in young horses).

---

**Pads, half-pads, and shims**

Pads are a between-fitting tool, not a fit correction. A correction-pocket sheepskin pad with shims (Mattes and similar) can carry small asymmetries that a qualified fitter has identified. A pad cannot fix structural wrongness — a saddle that bridges, sits twisted, or rocks under load needs saddle work (re-flock, re-fit, replace), not a thicker pad.

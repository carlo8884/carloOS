You signed up on Horses.com. Welcome.

This is a working horse-owner reference site — discipline notes, breed pages, supplement evaluations, health references, and equipment guides drawn from peer-reviewed equine veterinary literature and from public standards bodies (AAEP, NRC, SMS, AAEP-equivalents for dental and farriery). It is not a marketplace, not a clinical advice service, not a brand-owned content channel.

Over the next three weeks you will get four short emails, each on one decision real horse owners have to make — nutrition, supplements, saddle fit, preventive medicine — written from public sources and pointing you to the deeper reference pages on the site.

For anything specific to your horse, defer to an equine veterinarian. The site is reference; clinical decisions are your vet's.

---

**What lives on Horses.com today**

- **Health references.** Common conditions, the early-warning signs, when to call out a vet — colic, EGUS, laminitis, the major systemic diseases.
- **Supplements.** What each category actually does, when evidence supports it, when it doesn't. Joint, hoof, gut, vitamin-mineral.
- **Disciplines.** Reining, dressage, show jumping, eventing, trail, western pleasure — what each discipline asks of the horse and the equipment.
- **Breeds.** Practical breed pages — not pedigree theatre, just what each breed tends to be good at and where the soft spots are.
- **Guides.** Saddle fit basics, vaccination schedule, dental care — the working-owner essentials.

---

**What this email sequence will not do**

- Substitute for an equine vet.
- Tell you which brand of supplement is "best for your horse" — your horse is not on this email list and we don't know its bloodwork.
- Pretend a single sequence covers everything.

What it will do: anchor the four most-asked-about decision areas — nutrition, supplements, saddle fit, preventive medicine — to sources and to the Horses.com reference pages that go deeper.

— Horses.com Editorial

Boarding is the largest recurring expense in horse ownership and the biggest determinant of your day-to-day experience. Pick this well and almost everything else gets easier.

**The three common arrangements:**

- **Full board.** Stall, daily turnout, hay, grain (sometimes), water, mucking, blanket changes. Highest cost, lowest day-to-day labor. Common at training and show barns.
- **Self-care.** You provide and feed your own hay and grain, muck your own stall, manage your own turnout schedule. Lowest cost, highest time commitment — typically two visits a day, every day.
- **Pasture board.** Horse lives outside 24/7 with a herd, with run-in shelter, daily hay in season, water. Lowest cost of the boarded options, often the most natural lifestyle for the horse, fewer respiratory and ulcer issues — but you lose easy access to a clean indoor working space.

**The barn-tour checklist.** Walk through *before* signing. Walk through *unannounced* a second time if you can.

- **Footing.** Stall mats present and reasonably level. Arena footing maintained — not dust, not concrete, not deep sand.
- **Fencing.** Wood, vinyl, no-climb wire, or electric tape on suitable posts. No barbed wire on horse paddocks. No splintered or broken boards left in place.
- **Hay storage.** Dry, off the ground, rotated. Ask to see the current hay. Lift a flake.
- **Water.** Clean, accessible, ice-free in winter. Automatic waterers tested.
- **Manure management.** A real plan, not just a pile creeping toward the fence.
- **Turnout.** Adequate acreage per horse, group composition you can ask about, mud management.
- **Social dynamics.** Horses look settled, not frantic at the fence. Boarders speak to each other.
- **Manager responsiveness.** They answer the phone. They answer email. They tell you about a cut before you notice it.

**Red flags.** Any one of these is a question to ask; multiple is your answer.

- Closed-door tour ("we&apos;ll show you that part next time").
- Evasive answers about feeding schedule, turnout schedule, or who is on the property overnight.
- Sick-looking, thin, or under-attended horses in the herd you&apos;d be joining.
- Locked tack rooms with no signage about insurance or theft policy.
- High boarder turnover. (Ask current boarders, not the manager.)
- "Trainers only" arena access during the only hours you can ride.

**The contract.** Read it. Specifically: termination notice (30 days is common, 60 is not unusual), board increase notice, vet/farrier policies (some barns require their providers; you generally want the right to bring your own), liability, and what happens if you fall behind on board.

**The questions current boarders answer better than the manager.** When was the last time a horse got hurt and how was it handled? How quickly does the manager respond when you flag a problem? Have board rates gone up in the last year and how was it communicated?

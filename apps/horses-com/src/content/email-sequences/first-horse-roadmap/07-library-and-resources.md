Day 90. Last email in the series.

If you&apos;ve been following along, your horse has been home for about three months. The barn is working. The feed program is steady. The farrier and vet are on a cycle. The first-aid kit is on a shelf, not in a list. The ground manners are reflex. You know your horse&apos;s normal vitals from your own hands.

Most of what comes next is **keeping the program and going deeper as questions surface**. The library below is what to bookmark.

**Foundations to keep at hand**

- [American Quarter Horse breed guide](https://horses.com/breeds/quarter-horse) — relevant whether or not your first horse is a Quarter Horse; the conformation, soundness, and genetic-panel discussion applies broadly.
- [Equine gastric ulcers](https://horses.com/health/equine-ulcers) — disproportionately common in horses that have changed hands recently. If your horse develops sudden behavior changes, drops weight, or "girths up", this is the page.
- [Saddle-fit basics](https://horses.com/guides/saddle-fit-basics) — the four checkpoints, when to call a qualified fitter, and what the Society of Master Saddlers register actually means.

**Decisions you&apos;ll make in year one**

- [Joint supplements — evidence-graded ranking](https://horses.com/supplements/joint-supplements) — what has clinical evidence, what is marketing, and what to ask your vet before adding anything.
- [Best winter horse blankets](https://horses.com/reviews/best-winter-horse-blankets) — fit, denier, fill weight, and the right blanket for the climate and horse you actually have.

**The 90-day recap.**

1. **Pre-purchase exam.** Your vet, not the seller&apos;s. Thorough, breed- and use-appropriate.
2. **Boarding.** The tour, the contract, the boarders. Re-evaluate at six months.
3. **Vet + farrier.** Booked standing. Calendar-based, not crisis-based.
4. **Feed program.** Forage first. Gradual transitions. Body condition score every 4–6 weeks.
5. **Ground manners.** Whoa, walk on, back, over, stand. Reflex.
6. **First-aid kit + emergency plan.** Itemized. Posted. Updated.
7. **You.** Educated, organized, and able to recite your horse&apos;s vitals, feed, and contacts in your sleep.

**A note on the standards.** Across this series we&apos;ve cited the American Association of Equine Practitioners (AAEP), American Farriers Journal, Society of Master Saddlers, and American Horse Council. Where guidance differs between a confident voice on a forum and a sourced body, the body wins. Confidence is not credentialing.

Your vet, your farrier, your trainer, and your own observations of *your* horse beat any opinion on the internet, including this one. We try to send you to good sources. You make the calls.

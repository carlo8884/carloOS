Your **Pet Emergency Triage Card** is ready:

[Download the PDF →](https://vets.co/downloads/pet-emergency-triage-card.pdf)

The card is single-page. It belongs on the fridge or inside a cabinet door, not in a binder you cannot find at 2 a.m.

It covers, in order:

1. **Red-flag symptoms** that mean "go to the ER now, do not call first": collapse, non-stop vomiting, distended abdomen with retching, difficulty breathing, suspected poisoning, sudden hind-end paralysis, eye injury, active bleeding that does not slow under pressure in 5 minutes, seizures lasting longer than 3 minutes or clustered, heat-stroke signs.

2. **Yellow-flag symptoms** that warrant a same-day vet call: limping that worsens within hours, decreased appetite for more than 24 hours, single vomit + lethargy, blood in stool, squinting one eye, drinking visibly more than usual for more than 2 days.

3. **Vital signs to take and write down before you call**: temperature (rectal, °F), heart rate (bpm), respiratory rate (breaths per minute), gum colour, capillary refill time. The vet on the phone needs these numbers.

4. **Poison control phone numbers** and the bite-size protocols for the most common household toxins.

The card is a triage tool. It does not replace your vet. It tells you whether to dial them or drive.

Over the next two weeks, four short emails — each one focused on one thing the card could not fit on a single page.

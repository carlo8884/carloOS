Final email in this series.

Over the past three months, we've covered:

1. **The Pet Emergency Triage Card** — the three categories (ER NOW / same-day / monitor) and species-specific vital sign ranges
2. **The first-aid kit** — what to stock and why each item
3. **Taking vitals at home** — HR, RR, temp, CRT, gum colour by species
4. **Top 5 emergencies per species** — GDV, antifreeze, urethral obstruction, lily, string, and the others
5. **Telehealth** — when a $30 call covers the same-day vet category
6. **Pet insurance** — the catastrophic-event tool, with claim-payout context
7. **Senior pet planning** — shifted baselines, conservative ER thresholds, advance conversations

If you internalized half of that, you are dramatically more prepared than the average pet owner. The point of this series was never to make you an emergency vet — it was to make you a faster, calmer, better-informed first responder for the animal in your house.

## The closing thought

The single most useful sentence in this entire series is the one from email #1: **most pet emergencies are not actual emergencies.**

The corollary: **the actual emergencies are the ones where seconds matter, and the calmer you are in those seconds, the better the outcome.**

A printed card on the fridge. A first-aid kit in the closet. A 30-second vital-sign measurement you've done a dozen times before. The ASPCA poison-control number in your phone. The name of a 24-hour ER you've already driven past once just to know where it is.

That's the whole framework. The next time it actually matters, you'll be glad you read this.

Thank you for trusting us with three months of your inbox. Take care of your animal.

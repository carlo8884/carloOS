The triage card tells you which numbers to collect. This email tells you how.

**Heart rate.** Place fingers on the inside of the upper hind leg, where the femoral artery runs. Count beats for 15 seconds. Multiply by 4.

- Dogs, small breed: 100–140 bpm at rest.
- Dogs, medium: 70–120 bpm.
- Dogs, large: 60–100 bpm.
- Cats: 140–220 bpm at rest. (Yes, that's normal.)
- A rate consistently above the upper bound, or below the lower bound, at rest, is reportable.

**Respiratory rate.** Watch the chest rise and fall when the animal is sleeping or resting calmly. Count breaths for 30 seconds. Multiply by 2.

- Dogs at rest: 10–30 breaths/min.
- Cats at rest: 20–30 breaths/min.
- A *resting* respiratory rate consistently above 40 in either species is one of the earliest signs of congestive heart failure, and is among the most useful at-home metrics in all of veterinary medicine.

**Capillary refill time and gum colour.** Lift the lip, press a fingertip to the gum, release. The blanched spot should refill pink within 2 seconds. Pale, white, yellow, blue, or grey gums — or a refill longer than 2 seconds — are emergent findings.

**Temperature** is rectal, with a digital thermometer. Normal range for dogs and cats: 100.5–102.5 °F. Above 103.5 °F or below 99.5 °F warrants a call.

**The one piece of equipment we think every household should own:** a pet-specific digital thermometer kept in a labelled drawer. The human ones work; the pet-specific ones are faster and quieter, which matters at 2 a.m. on a stressed dog.

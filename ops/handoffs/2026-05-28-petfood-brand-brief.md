PetFood.com is the **independent ingredient-and-brand reference** — the Consumer Reports of pet food. Not a store. Not a brand site. We score every commercial pet food on AAFCO completeness, ingredient sourcing transparency, recall history, and feeding outcomes. The brand must project **independence and technical authority** so it is trusted as a comparison engine, not mistaken for a single-brand storefront or an advertorial.

## 1. Positioning

PetFood.com is the independent ingredient-and-brand reference for cat and dog food in North America. The Consumer Reports of pet food. We are not a store, not a brand mouthpiece, not a vet directory, not a forum. We score every commercial pet food sold in the US on a consistent rubric: AAFCO completeness, ingredient sourcing and traceability, recall history, manufacturing standards, and observed feeding outcomes. Where Dog Food Advisor is advertorial-heavy and dog-only, where All About Pet Food is UK-leaning, and where Chewy and Petco exist to move inventory, PetFood.com exists to answer one question — "is this food good, and compared to what?" — with a transparent rubric, citations, and no financial relationship to the brands being scored.

### Compete-against (and how we differ)

- **Dog Food Advisor** — single-category (dogs), opaque scoring rationale, history of affiliate-driven ranking accusations. We are multi-category, transparent rubric, no affiliate weighting in scores.
- **All About Pet Food (UK)** — closest comp in independence and scoring philosophy; UK-leaning, dog-focused. We are North America first, dogs + cats equal weighting.
- **Chewy / Petco / Pet Supplies Plus** — stores. They sell. We don't.
- **Brand marketing sites (Hill's, Royal Canin, Purina Pro Plan)** — these are advertising surfaces, not evaluation. We evaluate them.
- **Reddit r/DogFood, r/CatFood** — community-driven, unverified. We synthesize community signal into citable scoring.
- **Tufts Petfoodology, AVMA, WSAVA guidelines** — these are our peer institutions, not competitors. We cite them; we don't replace them. Where they publish guidelines (e.g., WSAVA on selecting a pet food manufacturer), we operationalize those guidelines into a per-product score.

### What "independence" means in practice

- No paid brand placement in rankings. Ever. Hard rule.
- No accepting product samples in exchange for review. Bought at retail, receipts kept.
- Scoring rubric published in full and versioned. When the rubric changes, prior scores are re-run.
- Disclosure of any commercial relationships (affiliate links to retailers like Chewy are allowed; affiliate weighting on the score is not).

## 2. Voice

### Five adjectives (✓)

1. **Skeptical** — assume marketing claims are unproven until ingredient panel, manufacturer disclosure, or third-party data backs them up. "Holistic," "premium," "human-grade" are unregulated terms; we say so.
2. **Evidence-based** — every claim cites AAFCO, WSAVA, peer-reviewed nutrition research, FDA recall database, or manufacturer-confirmed sourcing. No vibes.
3. **Technical** — we use the correct term (taurine, not "amino acid stuff"; DCM, not "heart problems") and define it on first use.
4. **Transparent** — we show our work. Scoring formula visible. Methodology page linked from every score.
5. **Comparative** — every product is reviewed in context of its category competitors, not in isolation. "Good" is always "good compared to what."

### Five anti-adjectives (✗)

1. **Cheerleader** — no "we love this brand!" energy. We don't love brands. We score them.
2. **Brand-favoring** — no preferential treatment for legacy brands (Purina, Hill's) or boutique brands (Orijen, Open Farm). Same rubric applied identically.
3. **Fluffy** — no anecdotes about "my dog Cooper loves this kibble." We are not a blog.
4. **Anthropomorphizing** — no "Fido will love it!" / "your furry family member." Pets are pets.
5. **Fearmongering** — no "this ingredient is KILLING your dog." Risk is quantified, contextualized, and proportional to evidence.

### Voice examples

- ✓ "Acana Wild Atlantic is grain-free and fish-forward. Champion Petfoods (the manufacturer) is named in the FDA's 2019 DCM investigation, which examined a possible association between grain-free legume-heavy diets and dilated cardiomyopathy in dogs. The investigation did not establish causation but did not exonerate the category. Owners of breeds with elevated DCM risk (Doberman, Great Dane, Cocker Spaniel) should consult a board-certified veterinary cardiologist before feeding."
- ✗ "Acana makes amazing food! Sourced from regional ingredients, your pup will thank you. However, some recent studies suggest grain-free might be linked to heart issues — definitely worth chatting with your vet about!"

- ✓ "This formula meets AAFCO adult maintenance via formulation, not feeding trial. Formulation-only certification means the nutrient profile is calculated, not verified in living animals. Brands that complete feeding trials score higher on our methodology rubric (Section 3.2)."
- ✗ "This food is complete and balanced for adult dogs!"

## 3. Visual Direction

### Recommendation: clean food-science

Strip away pet-marketing visual tropes. The site should look closer to a scientific data publisher (Our World in Data, FiveThirtyEight, Consumer Reports) than to a pet brand.

- **Background:** warm white (off-white, not pure #FFFFFF). Slight cream warmth so the site doesn't feel sterile.
- **Primary accent:** choose one. Carlo decides.
  - **Option A — warm orange (scaffold):** read as approachable, food-coded. Risk: conflates with Chewy's brand orange and Petco's color family. Mitigation: use sparingly, only for CTAs and scoring badges.
  - **Option B — deep moss green:** reads as earned trust, scientific, independent. Differentiates from competitor-orange. Recommendation: lean here.
- **Text:** very dark warm-gray (not pure black). Pure black on warm white reads as harsh.
- **Data visualization palette:** restrained — accent color + two neutrals (warm gray, cool gray). Charts must not look "playful." No cartoon kibble icons in chart legends.
- **Whitespace:** generous. The site should breathe like a research publication, not pack like an e-commerce category page.
- **Scoring badge system:** numeric (e.g., 7.4 / 10) on a colored chip — green for high, neutral gray for mid, muted red for low. Colorblind-safe palette mandatory; pair color with a glyph (filled vs hollow vs ringed circle).

### What to avoid

- **Cartoon pet illustrations** — paw prints, dog silhouettes, kibble-bowl icons. These signal "pet blog" and undercut independence.
- **AI-generated kibble bowls** — uncanny-valley food, generic, indistinguishable from competitor sites.
- **Marketing-y product mockups** — soft-focus product photography against gradient backgrounds. We are not selling the product.
- **Manufacturer-provided imagery** — bag mockups, hero shots. Using these reads as endorsement.
- **"Trust badges" of dubious provenance** — gold stars, "vet recommended" without a named vet.
- **Bright primary palette** — kid's-toy red/yellow/blue reads as Petco-aisle, not Consumer Reports.

### Reference brands

- **Consumer Reports** — the philosophical and visual benchmark. Score badges, comparison rows, restrained palette.
- **Our World in Data** — data-forward layout, generous whitespace, scientific without being cold.
- **Wirecutter (early era, pre-NYT-redesign)** — comparison-first content with embedded rationale.
- **Cook's Illustrated / America's Test Kitchen** — methodology-visible, evidence-led, no brand cheerleading.
- **OpenFoodFacts** — open-data product database aesthetic; we're more curated and editorial than they are.

## 4. Typography

### Recommendation

- **Display / category headlines:** Cormorant (as scaffolded) — keep. High-contrast serif gives the site editorial weight without going corporate. Use only at large sizes (28px+).
- **Body:** Inter — keep. Workhorse sans, excellent at small sizes, full weight range, supports tabular figures (critical for our scoring tables).
- **Monospace:** **add JetBrains Mono** for ingredient tables, data callouts, scoring formulas, and methodology references. Monospace signals "this is data, not marketing copy."
- Three families, no more. Pick them, lock them.

### What to avoid

- **Rounded humanist sans** (Nunito, Quicksand) — pet-blog default. Undercuts authority.
- **Display fonts in body** — Cormorant below 18px destroys readability.
- **Mixing serif body with serif display** — pick a clean break (serif display, sans body).
- **Variable-weight novelty effects** — animated weight shifts on hover, etc. We are a reference, not a portfolio.
- **System-default sans (Helvetica/Arial/-apple-system)** — generic; ships a brand-incoherent default.

### Type system (proposed)

| Use | Family | Weight | Size (desktop) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 (article) | Cormorant | 600 | 48px |
| H2 | Cormorant | 600 | 32px |
| H3 | Inter | 700 | 20px |

| Body emphasis | Inter | 600 | 17px |

## 5. Hero Imagery Direction

### Recommendation

**Ingredient photography and scientific transparency.** Close-ups of actual raw ingredients — chicken meal in a petri dish, salmon oil being poured, taurine crystals at macro scale. Where products appear, they sit next to their ingredient sources (a bag of kibble next to a pile of the listed protein source, raw and unstyled). Lab-bench staging: stainless steel, neutral backgrounds, even lighting.

### Technical guidelines

- **Lighting:** even, daylight-balanced. No moody food-styling shadows; this is data, not a restaurant menu.
- **Composition:** centered, symmetrical, minimal. Subject isolated against neutral background.
- **Color grading:** true-to-life. No warm filter, no Instagram preset. Ingredients should look like ingredients, not stylized food.
- **Subject:** one ingredient or one product. Never lifestyle.
- **Treatment:** clinical but not cold. Think "Cook's Illustrated test kitchen" not "supplement-company white-background marketing."

### What to avoid (NO list)

- **Smiling-pet-with-bowl stock photos** — anti-credible, pet-blog default.
- **Brand product hero shots from manufacturer marketing** — that's their image, not ours; using it reads as endorsement.
- **Lifestyle "happy dog on hike" imagery** — irrelevant to a food-scoring site; signals lifestyle blog, not reference.
- **AI-generated kibble or pet imagery** — generic, hard rule, no.
- **Stock-photo veterinarians in white coats** — staged, anti-credible.
- **Cartoon ingredient illustrations** — children's-book register.

### Photography sourcing

- Commission an ingredient-reference library: every common ingredient (meat meals, grains, legumes, vitamins, preservatives) photographed in raw form. One-time cost, decade-long shelf life. This becomes a moat.
- Product photography: buy products at retail, photograph in-house against neutral background. Never use manufacturer imagery.
- Avoid licensed stock entirely for hero placement. Licensed stock acceptable for ancillary editorial illustration only if heavily curated.

## 6. Signature Content Patterns

These are the recurring layout/voice motifs that make the site feel like itself.

### Brand-comparison tables

- Every brand-level review includes a comparison table vs 3-5 peers in the same category (e.g., "grain-inclusive premium dog dry"). Same rubric, same dimensions, sortable.

### Ingredient-by-ingredient scoring

- The flagship signature feature. For every formula, we display the full ingredient panel in monospace, annotated with our notes per ingredient — sourcing, regulatory status, nutritional rationale, common concerns. This is the moat. No competitor does this systematically.

### Recall timeline graphics

- Every brand page shows a horizontal timeline of FDA recalls and voluntary withdrawals affecting that brand or its manufacturer. Pulled from FDA CVM database. Citation linked.

### "Ingredients Glossary"

- Demystifies common-but-confusing entries: taurine, BHA/BHT, "natural flavors," guar gum, ethoxyquin, methionine, tocopherols, beet pulp, brewer's rice, glucosamine, chondroitin, sodium tripolyphosphate.
- Each glossary entry: what it is, why it's in pet food, regulatory status, safety profile, evidence quality, our scoring impact.
- Cross-linked from every ingredient panel.

### Methodology page (versioned)

- Linked from every score, every page. Versioned (v1.0, v1.1, etc.) so readers can see when the rubric changed and how. When we re-score, we publish a changelog.

### Veterinary disclaimer

- On every health-adjacent article: "PetFood.com is reference material. Therapeutic diets, allergies, and breed-specific risks require a veterinarian. If your pet is showing [symptoms], call your vet."

### "Last reviewed" timestamps

- Every score shows last-reviewed date. Re-scored on a published cadence (annually minimum; immediately on recall or formula change).

### Affiliate disclosure (per-page)

- Where retailer affiliate links exist (e.g., "buy this on Chewy"), inline disclosure: "PetFood.com earns a commission from retailers when you click through. We do not accept payment from food brands and our scores are not influenced by affiliate relationships."

## 7. Do-NOT-do List

- **No recommending raw diets without DCM-risk + bacterial-risk disclosure.** Raw is a legitimate category but carries real risks (salmonella, listeria, taurine-deficiency profiles in some recipes). Every raw mention carries the disclosure.
- **No taking brand payment for favorable scores.** Hard rule. Termination-level violation if anyone proposes it.
- **No using manufacturer marketing images.** We shoot our own product photography. Their imagery on our site implies endorsement.
- **No anti-grain fearmongering.** Grain-inclusive diets are appropriate for most dogs. Grain-free is a category, not a virtue. Our scoring reflects evidence, not the marketing-driven grain-free panic of 2014-2018.
- **No anti-by-product fearmongering.** "By-product" is an AAFCO-defined term covering nutritious organ meats. We explain it; we don't scare-quote it.
- **No anthropomorphic copy.** "Fido will love it!" "Your fur baby deserves the best!" "Treat your best friend!" None of it.
- **No "best of" rankings without rubric.** Every ranking ties back to the methodology page.
- **No prescriptive medical advice.** Allergies, IBD, kidney disease, urinary issues — we describe diet options but defer to vets for prescription.
- **No accepting "advisory board" positions from brands.** Tufts and WSAVA's research is fair to cite; an "advisory board" seat at a brand is a conflict.
- **No private-label food product** unless explicitly and separately walled off — selling our own food would compromise the entire scoring premise. (Defer to monetization brief; recommendation here is hard no.)
- **No "vet recommended" badges** unless we name the vet and their relationship.
- **No undisclosed AI content.** AI may draft; humans review, sign, and own the byline.
- **No clickbait recall headlines.** Recalls are reported with proportion and context: scope, affected lots, severity, FDA classification.
- **No comments section.** Moderation cost exceeds value; misinformation in comments contaminates the page's authority signal.

## 8. Brand Architecture (proposed)

- **Wordmark:** "PetFood.com" in Inter, weight 700, with the period as an intentional element (accent-colored). Sans wordmark, not serif — signals "reference / utility" over "editorial."
- **No mascot.** No cartoon dog, no paw print. The wordmark is the mark.
- **Favicon:** the period, in accent color.
- **Possible sub-brand modifiers** (see open questions):
  - PetFood.com **Index** — the scoring database (potentially the highest-value sub-brand; the "Index" framing positions us as the canonical source).
  - PetFood.com **Scorecard** — per-product scoring page convention.
  - PetFood.com **Recall Tracker** — the FDA-recall-database surface.
  - PetFood.com **Glossary** — the ingredient encyclopedia.

Recommendation: **lead with "Index"** as the master sub-brand for the scoring database. "The PetFood.com Index" is citable, repeatable, and positions us linguistically as the standard reference.

## 9. Open Questions for Carlo

1. **Accent color** — orange (scaffold) or moss green (recommendation)? Pick one before content scales.
2. **Sub-brand modifier** — PetFood.com Index? PetFood.com Scorecard? Recommendation: Index for the database, Scorecard for individual product pages.
3. **Proprietary scoring engine** — do we build the scoring rubric as a proprietary data moat (versioned methodology, in-house tooling, eventual API)? Recommendation: yes — the scoring engine is the entire defensibility story. Without it we're a blog.
4. **Photography budget** — commission an ingredient-reference library now (one-time, decade-long shelf life, real moat) or license + opportunistic commissioning? Recommendation: commission ingredient library in Q3. Estimated 80-120 hero ingredient shots covers ~90% of common formulations.
5. **Cat vs dog editorial weighting** — dog content has higher search volume; cat content has less competition and higher per-page revenue potential. Recommendation: launch dog + cat at equal coverage depth from day one. Cat is underserved; we win there faster.
6. **Veterinary advisory board** — do we recruit board-certified veterinary nutritionists (ACVIM-Nutrition diplomates) as formal advisors? Recommendation: yes, but small (2-3) and compensated. Their credentialing makes our health claims more defensible.
7. **Affiliate strategy** — retailer affiliate only (Chewy, Amazon, Petco) or also DTC brands? Recommendation: retailer-affiliate only. Brand-DTC affiliate creates a per-brand financial relationship that compromises scoring credibility, even if internal walls exist.
8. **International scope** — US first; UK, Canada, Australia later? AAFCO is US-specific; FEDIAF (EU) is different. Recommendation: US-first, no apologies, expand only when US is dominant in category.
9. **Database licensing** — when the Index is mature, do we license the data to vets, retailers, insurers? Recommendation: yes, as a secondary revenue line. Don't pre-build it; build the Index first, license later when the data is sought.
10. **Sponsorship policy** — accept any brand sponsorship? Recommendation: no, full stop. Independence is the brand. Sponsorship contaminates it. Revenue comes from affiliate, data licensing, and (eventually) paid subscription tiers for power-user features.

## 11. Risks

- **Risk:** brand looks "too clinical" and loses casual-searcher audience.
  - **Mitigation:** voice stays plainspoken even when content is technical. Hero imagery is restrained but warm (cream background, not pure white; ingredient close-ups, not lab coats).
- **Risk:** "Consumer Reports of pet food" framing is high promise; sub-par execution destroys it fast.
  - **Mitigation:** launch with depth in fewer categories rather than thin coverage everywhere. Better to ship 50 thorough scorecards than 500 shallow ones.
- **Risk:** legal exposure from negative scoring of named brands.
  - **Mitigation:** every claim cited; methodology transparent and applied uniformly; named legal review of category launches.
- **Risk:** affiliate revenue from Chewy/Amazon creates pressure to soften scores on highly-purchased products.
  - **Mitigation:** scoring engine is rubric-driven, not editor-discretion. Affiliate revenue is reported quarterly to a small editorial-independence committee (even if that committee is currently just Carlo).
- **Risk:** AI-generated content arms race floods the space with low-quality competitors.
  - **Mitigation:** the moat is the data (scoring engine, ingredient library, recall database), not the prose. Competitors can write articles; they can't replicate the data without years of work.

## TL;DR

Horses.com is the **category-defining knowledge hub** for horse owners across disciplines. It is not a magazine, not a shop, not a blog. Visual identity is **refined equestrian** — leather, cream, deep forest green, brass accents. Voice is **vet-respectful and plainspoken**. Monetization tilts equestrian-luxury (SmartPak, Dover, performance feeds), so the brand must read as authoritative enough to be trusted by readers who are about to spend $400 on a saddle pad or $1,200 on a year of joint supplements.

The placeholder palette (warm browns / cream / dark green) is directionally correct and **should be confirmed**, with one refinement: replace any muddy mid-brown with a saddle-leather tan, and treat dark green as the primary brand color rather than a tertiary.

## 1. Positioning

Horses.com is the authoritative reference for horse owners across discipline lines — English, Western, trail, racing, eventing, driving. Not a magazine. Not a shop. Not a community forum. The category-defining knowledge hub: every gear category, every supplement, every common ailment, every training concept, evaluated with the same rigor a veterinary textbook would apply but written for the owner who actually has to make the decision at 7am before the farrier arrives. Where Practical Horseman runs features and Dover sells product, Horses.com explains what the product is, when it matters, and which discipline it belongs to.

### Compete-against (and how we differ)

- **Practical Horseman / Horse & Hound / EQUUS** — magazines. News, features, personalities, op-eds. We are reference, not editorial calendar. They publish; we maintain.
- **SmartPak / Dover Saddlery / Schneiders** — shops. They sell. We don't. Our value is the buyer's-guide layer they cannot credibly own.
- **Generic horse blogs / TheHorse.com** — TheHorse.com is the closest comp on veterinary content but is paywalled, dated UX, and weak on gear/discipline coverage. Generic blogs are SEO-thin and brand-incoherent.
- **Reddit r/Equestrian / COTH forums** — community-driven, unverified, hard to navigate. We synthesize what the community already knows into a citable source.

### What "authoritative reference" means in practice

- Every claim is sourced (veterinary journal, AAEP, breed registry, FEI rule book, manufacturer spec sheet).
- Every gear review is discipline-tagged so an English rider doesn't get Western recommendations by accident.
- Every health article carries a veterinary disclaimer and points to "when to call your vet."
- The site reads the same whether you arrived via a Google search for "horse colic symptoms" or "best dressage saddle under 3000."

## 2. Voice

### Five adjectives (✓)

1. **Confident** — we know the answer; we don't hedge for politeness. If a product is overpriced, we say so. If a training trend is unsupported, we say so.
2. **Grounded** — written by people who have mucked stalls. We reference real conditions (mud, winter, a horse who pulls), not idealized arena scenarios.
3. **Vet-respectful** — we defer to veterinary authority on medical questions and signal where lay opinion ends and clinical judgment begins.
4. **Technical** — we use the correct term (cannon bone, not "lower leg"; trot diagonal, not "bumpy gait") and then define it inline.
5. **Plainspoken** — technical does not mean academic. Sentences are short. Verbs are active. The reader is treated as an intelligent adult who happens not to be a vet.

### Five anti-adjectives (✗)

1. **Folksy** — no "Howdy, partner" cowboy register. No "your four-legged friend." No "ol' Bessie."
2. **Marketing-y** — no "revolutionary," "game-changing," "must-have." We don't sell; we explain.
3. **Jargon-heavy** — technical terms appear, but undefined jargon does not. If we say "navicular," we explain it.
4. **Opinion-led** — we lead with evidence, not personality. No first-person "I think." Bylines exist for accountability, not for column-building.
5. **Hype** — no superlatives without data. "The best saddle pad" is a comparison conclusion, never a headline.

### Voice examples

- ✓ "A deep digital flexor injury rarely heals fully. Conservative management — stall rest, controlled exercise, shockwave — restores work in roughly 60% of cases (AAEP, 2024). The remainder require surgical intervention or retirement."
- ✗ "Tendon injuries can be scary, but don't worry — with the right care, your equine partner will be back in the saddle in no time!"

- ✓ "For an English-discipline rider doing one-hour schooling sessions on a sound horse, a wool-felt half pad is overkill. A correctly-fit saddle on a clean cotton pad does the same job."
- ✗ "Treat your horse to the luxury he deserves with our top wool-felt half pad picks!"

## 3. Visual Direction

### Recommendation: refined equestrian

The scaffold placeholder (warm browns + cream + dark green) is **directionally correct**. Confirm with these refinements:

- **Primary:** deep forest green (think: hunt coat, Hermès equestrian catalog spine). Use as nav, headings, primary CTA.
- **Secondary:** saddle-leather tan (not a muddy brown — think a well-oiled English bridle, not a Western chap).
- **Background:** warm cream / unbleached paper. Never pure white.
- **Accent:** brass / antique gold for small UI moments — rule lines under headings, bullet markers, micro-icons. Used sparingly. This is the detail that signals "premium" without saying it.
- **Text:** very dark green-black (not pure #000) for body. Pure black reads as web-default; the off-black reads as printed.

### What to avoid

- **Barn-rustic clichés:** red plaid, faux-distressed wood textures, rope-typography flourishes, horseshoe motifs in headers.
- **Western-saloon palette:** sun-bleached terracotta, turquoise, ochre. These read as a specific discipline, not the cross-discipline authority we are.
- **Hospital-clinical:** pure white + cool blue + sans-serif everywhere reads as a vet clinic site. We are warmer than that.
- **Country-living-magazine:** sage green + blush pink + script. That's a different audience.

### Reference brands

- **Hermès equestrian** — restraint, leather, cream, deep saturated brand color. The benchmark for premium without ostentation.
- **Pikeur / Eskadron catalogues** — clean, photographic, dark backgrounds, generous whitespace around product.
- **Dressage Today (print era, pre-2018)** — long-form layouts, serif body, photo-led, no clutter.
- **Mark Todd / Charles Owen brand sites** — restrained, English-discipline-coded, no rotating carousel nonsense.

## 4. Typography

### Recommendation

- **Display / headings:** Playfair Display (as scaffolded) — keep. High-contrast didone-adjacent serif reads as equestrian-print-tradition without going full Vogue. Use at sizes that let the contrast breathe (32px and up; below that the thin strokes vanish on Android).
- **Body:** Source Sans 3 (as scaffolded) is fine; Inter is a stronger alternative for long-form readability. Either works. Pick one and stop touching it.
- **UI / labels:** same as body, downsized. Do not introduce a third family.
- **Monospace:** none needed unless we build a tabular calculator (e.g., feed-ration tool). Defer.

### What to avoid

- **Scripts and faux-handwriting** — "Saddle Up Sans" energy. No.
- **Distressed / letterpress-imitation fonts** — reads as Pinterest farmhouse, not equestrian authority.
- **Western-saloon serifs** (anything resembling Wanted-poster type) — discipline-coded wrong way.
- **Display fonts in body copy** — Playfair below ~18px destroys readability.
- **Variable-weight gimmicks** — no weight-cycling hover effects. This is a reference site, not a portfolio.

### Type system (proposed)

| Use | Family | Weight | Size (desktop) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Playfair Display | 700 | 56px |
| H2 | Playfair Display | 700 | 36px |
| H3 | Playfair Display | 600 | 24px |
| Body | Source Sans 3 | 400 | 18px / 1.6 line-height |
| Body emphasis | Source Sans 3 | 600 | 18px |
| Caption | Source Sans 3 | 400 | 14px |
| Pull quote | Playfair Display | 400 italic | 28px |

## 5. Hero Imagery Direction

### Recommendation

**Moody, single-subject, environmental.** A horse standing in early-morning fog. A close-up of a hand checking a girth. A pair of mud-streaked tall boots by a tack-room door. The image tells the reader: this site is written by someone who has been there.

### Technical guidelines

- **Lighting:** golden hour, blue hour, or overcast. Avoid harsh midday.
- **Composition:** rule of thirds; subject off-center; significant negative space (we'll overlay type).
- **Color grading:** warm shadows, low saturation. Should sit naturally on the cream background without color clash.
- **Subject:** one horse or one rider or one piece of gear. Never crowds, never "team" shots.
- **Environment:** real barns, real arenas, real trails. Show mud, weathered wood, worn leather. Not pristine.

### What to avoid (NO list)

- Stock-photo "smiling rider hugging horse" — uncanny, overused, anti-credible.
- Generic green-pasture-with-blue-sky — looks like every other horse site, signals nothing.
- "Inspirational" silhouette-against-sunset — Pinterest-tier, reads as low-effort.
- AI-generated horse imagery — horse anatomy is uncanny-valley-prone (extra legs, fused fetlocks, impossible musculature). Hard rule: no AI images of horses anywhere on the site.
- Manufacturer marketing shots (saddle catalogs, supplement bottle hero) — reads as advertorial.

### Photography sourcing

- Commission. Long-term: a small roster of 3-5 photographers (one per major discipline region — English-East-Coast, Western-Mountain-West, UK-based for international flavor).
- Short-term: high-end licensed (Stocksy, Trunk Archive) over Getty/Shutterstock.
- Build an in-house library for evergreen content (anatomy diagrams, gear close-ups, ailment reference photos). These become a moat.

## 6. Signature Content Patterns

These are the recurring layout/voice motifs that make the site feel like itself across categories.

### Comparison tables

- Every gear category gets a comparison table: 4-8 products, scored on the same dimensions (fit, durability, value, discipline-fit).
- Sortable. Discipline-filterable. Linked to a "how we test" page.
- Use the monospace-adjacent tabular figures option in Source Sans 3 so numbers align.

### Discipline-aware callouts

- A small inline component: **"For English riders:" / "For Western riders:" / "For trail riders:"**
- Used wherever the answer differs by discipline. Don't bury the distinction in prose.

### Peer-reviewed citation

- Health and veterinary articles cite specifically: journal, year, authors. Inline numbered citations, footnoted at the bottom.
- Builds Google E-E-A-T signal and reader trust simultaneously.

### Veterinary disclaimer

- Standard disclaimer block on every health article: "This article is reference material, not veterinary advice. If your horse is showing [symptoms], call your vet."
- Placed near top of article, not buried in footer.

### "Last reviewed" timestamps

- Every article shows last-reviewed date and reviewer name. Reference content has to feel maintained.

### Glossary cross-links

- Technical terms link to a glossary entry on first use per article. Builds internal link graph and reduces bounce when a reader hits an unfamiliar term.

### Author bios with credentials

- DVMs, certified trainers, FEI judges. Credentials visible inline. No anonymous content.

## 7. Do-NOT-do List

- **No "Cowboy Up" / "Saddle Up" / "Giddy Up" type slogans.** Discipline-coded wrong, voice-coded folksy.
- **No aggregating other people's content.** No "10 best blog posts about colic" link roundups. We synthesize from primary sources, not from other websites.
- **No clickbait headlines.** "You won't BELIEVE what this farrier discovered." No. The headline describes the article.
- **No AI-generated horse images.** Anywhere. Hard rule. Anatomy fails are reputationally fatal in this niche.
- **No AI-generated horse content presented as authored.** AI drafts may exist internally; published bylines are human.
- **No anthropomorphizing.** Horses are not "fur babies." They are horses. They have needs, not feelings-we-can-project.
- **No discipline tribalism.** We don't bash Western from an English perspective or vice versa.
- **No undisclosed affiliate placement.** If a product link earns commission, that's disclosed at the top of the article.
- **No "sponsored by" content masquerading as editorial.** Sponsored content is labeled and visually distinct.
- **No "Top 10" SEO-bait without genuine comparative testing.** If we publish a ranking, we tested or we cite who did.
- **No paywalls on health content.** Reference utility is the brand. Monetize through commerce affiliate, not gating.
- **No comments section.** Moderation cost exceeds value. Community lives on Reddit / COTH; we don't try to compete.

## 8. Brand Architecture (proposed)

- **Wordmark:** "Horses.com" in Playfair Display, all lowercase, with the period as an intentional design element (not an afterthought). The dot is small, brass-colored, sits on the baseline.
- **No mascot.** No silhouetted horse-head logo. The wordmark is the mark.
- **Favicon:** the period alone, brass on cream.
- **Possible sub-brand modifiers** (see open questions):
  - Horses.com **Reference** — the encyclopedic core.
  - Horses.com **Editorial** — long-form, opinion-acknowledged pieces.
  - Horses.com **Reviews** — gear comparison engine.
  - Horses.com **Vet** — veterinary content vertical with stricter review standards.

## 9. Open Questions for Carlo

1. **Brand modifier strategy** — do we want sub-brand modifiers (Horses.com Reference, Horses.com Editorial, Horses.com Reviews) or is it all one undifferentiated site? Recommendation: launch as one site, introduce modifiers only if/when we add categories that need a different trust contract (e.g., a community/forum section).
2. **Logotype direction** — confirm "set Playfair, don't draw a custom mark" or do you want to commission a wordmark? Cost difference is meaningful; brand-payoff is marginal at this stage.
3. **Photographer relationships** — do we commission now (build a moat early) or license + commission opportunistically (cheaper, slower)? Recommendation: commission a small first set (10-15 hero images, anatomy reference set) within Q3.
4. **Discipline emphasis weighting** — English is the highest-CPM discipline (luxury gear, dressage/show jumping spend). Western is the largest US population. Do we lead English (revenue) or balance evenly (audience)? Recommendation: lead English visually because it sets the premium tone; cover Western and trail with equal editorial rigor so we don't lose the long tail.
5. **Veterinary advisory board** — formal or informal? A named board of 3-5 DVMs adds enormous E-E-A-T weight, but adds review-cycle latency. Recommendation: yes, formal, named, compensated for review hours.
6. **Affiliate-vs-direct-commerce** — confirmed affiliate-only (per monetization brief)? No private label, no marketplace?
7. **International scope** — Horses.com vs Horses.co.uk content split. UK/Ireland is a meaningful equestrian market with different brand vocabulary. Recommendation: launch US-English; add UK-English variant pages for evergreen content within 12 months.
8. **Sponsorship policy** — will we accept brand sponsorship of category pages? Recommendation: no on category pages (kills the comparison credibility); yes on event coverage and clearly-labeled features.

## 11. Risks

- **Risk:** brand reads as "English-only" and loses the Western/trail audience.
  - **Mitigation:** discipline-callout component used aggressively; Western-focused hero imagery rotated into homepage.
- **Risk:** monetization tilt to luxury equestrian (SmartPak, Dover) pulls voice toward advertorial.
  - **Mitigation:** affiliate disclosure hard rules; comparison tables published with non-affiliate options included.
- **Risk:** veterinary advisory board adds latency that slows content velocity.
  - **Mitigation:** tier review process — vet review required only for clinical content, not gear or training.
- **Risk:** photography budget balloons before traffic justifies it.
  - **Mitigation:** commission anatomy / evergreen reference first (long shelf life); license editorial hero imagery until traffic supports staff/commission.

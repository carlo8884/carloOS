## TL;DR

Horses.com should monetize as an **affiliate-led editorial hub** for the first 12 months, not as a marketplace, not as an ad-supported publisher, and not as a lead-gen broker. The equine vertical is high-AOV (saddles $1.5K-5K, supplements $40-120/mo, trailers $10K-60K), low-CPC (Google ads pay poorly here), and dominated by a handful of beloved retailers (SmartPak, Dover, Riding Warehouse) who run mature affiliate programs. The category-defining domain gives Horses.com a structural advantage in buyer-intent SEO (e.g., "best winter horse blanket 2026", "SmartPak vs Dover", "ulcer supplement comparison") which converts at 2-6% on retailer programs with 5-12% typical commissions. Year 1 target: $80K-180K net revenue from affiliate + 1-2 insurance lead-gen partnerships, fueled by 60-120 high-intent buyer-guide articles and a comparison-table data layer. Do NOT build a marketplace, do NOT chase display ad RPMs, do NOT try to out-publish Practical Horseman on news. Year 3 the marketplace pivot becomes viable once we own the audience and have purchase-intent data.

## TAM rough sizing

- **US equine industry total**: ~$177B economic impact (American Horse Council 2023); ~7.2M horses; ~38M Americans participate annually as riders, owners, or spectators.
- **Active horse owners (direct addressable)**: ~2M households, ASP $4K-9K/yr on horse-related goods & services (feed, hay, vet, farrier, tack, apparel, insurance, trailering). Total annual owner spend ~$8B-15B in the addressable consumer slice (excludes racing/breeding institutional spend).
- **Online-influenced spend**: ~$3B-5B (estimate; equine consumer is older skewing 40-60, but mobile-discovery has hit ~70%+).
- **E-commerce equine retail GMV** (SmartPak, Dover, Schneider, Riding Warehouse, Tractor Supply equine, Chewy equine, Amazon equine): rough $1.5B-2.5B.
- **Realistic addressable share for a category-defining content site at maturity (Yr 3-5)**: 0.5%-2% of online-influenced spend = $15M-100M influenced GMV; at blended 7% affiliate commission that's $1M-7M revenue ceiling. Yr 1 we capture <5% of that ceiling = $80K-180K realistic.
- **Lead-gen sub-market**: equine insurance ~$120M-200M annual US premium; mortality/major-medical leads worth $25-80 each at retail; equine vet telehealth nascent ~$5M-15M GMV, leads $15-40 each.

### Model deep-dive

**1. Affiliate.** The equine retail commission landscape favors content sites that drive buyer-intent traffic to a small set of beloved retailers. The reader is researching ulcer supplements, winter blankets, well-fitting saddles, or trailering hay nets, and the merchant pays out 4-12% of cart on a 14-60 day cookie. AOVs in the category are high — a winter blanket buyer's average order is $180-320; a saddle buyer's average order is $1500-4000. A page that ranks #1-3 for "best winter horse blanket" can do $40-150 RPM in a way that no display ad layout can match. Limitations: programs can throttle commissions during peak season, retailers occasionally pull category from affiliate eligibility (especially clearance), and a handful of high-AOV categories (custom saddles, custom trailers) sit outside online retail entirely.

**2. Lead-gen.** Equine insurance is the headline. ~10 active brokers nationally, $25-80 per qualified mortality / major-medical lead at retail, and almost no aggregator competition for equine-specific search ("how much does horse insurance cost", "best mortality insurance for performance horses"). The pages that monetize lead-gen at $80-300 RPM are narrow — they require strong intent ("get a quote", "compare policies") and direct broker partnerships, not just affiliate-network click-out. Adjacent verticals: trailer financing (loan brokers will pay for qualified credit-app starts), equine real estate, equine vet telehealth. Patience required — direct partnerships take 4-12 weeks to paper.

**3. Sponsorship / brand integrations.** Once we have 50K+ monthly engaged sessions, branded content deals with Ariat, Kerrits, KER, Stubben, and the major feed brands become available at $5K-40K per quarter. This is a Yr 2+ revenue line in practice — brand buyers want audience proof before committing.

**4. Subscription.** Possible utility moats: a vet-AI symptom checker, a "feeding calculator" with personalized supplement recommendations, a board-facility finder with verified reviews, a training-plan library. Subscription revenue is sticky but conversion rates are typically <2%, so it's a build-on-top-of-audience play, not a primary engine.

**5-8.** Marketplace, display, licensing, private label — discussed in the Anti-recommendation section.

## Marketplace opportunity

**Categories to consider** (in order of attractiveness):
1. Used tack (saddles, bridles, blankets) — high search volume, low listing-creation friction, Facebook Marketplace + Tack Trader currently dominant but UX is poor.
2. Lease / part-board listings — local, recurring, underserved by clean software.
3. Horse sales — large GMV but very high trust burden, scams are endemic (wire fraud, misrepresented soundness), DreamHorse and BigEq own the high-end show market.
4. Boarding facility directory — moderate complexity, natural extension of board lead-gen.

**Defensibility**: High once liquidity is achieved. Horse buyers are loyal to whichever marketplace shows them the most relevant listings. Network effect is real but slow.

**Complexity**: High. Need trust & safety (especially for horse sales — vet check coordination, escrow), payments infrastructure (Stripe Connect minimum), photo upload moderation, search/filter UX, dispute handling. Realistic MLP is a 4-6 month build with 1 engineer + 1 community manager.

**Capital needs**: $80K-200K to reach a credible MLP (mostly engineering + content moderation labor). Seed liquidity may require paid "founding seller" recruitment ($5K-25K).

**Recommendation**: **Defer to Yr 2.** Build affiliate audience first; use audience data (search queries for "saddle for sale", "lease horse near me") to validate which marketplace vertical has the most pull. Most-likely MLP is used-tack — lowest trust burden, highest transaction frequency, fastest path to learning.

### Marketplace Yr-1 instrumentation (cheap, optional)

Even if we don't build a marketplace, we should capture marketplace-validation data in Yr 1 at near-zero cost:

- A "Wantads" / "Classifieds" page where readers can submit listings via a simple form (manual moderation, no payments, no escrow). Cost: ~1 day of build + 1-2 hrs/week of moderation.
- Track listing-submission volume by category (saddle, blanket, trailer, lease, board) — that's our first read on supply.
- Track listing-view to outbound-contact-click ratio — that's our first read on demand.
- If by M9 we have 200+ listings/mo with 5%+ contact-click rate on used-tack specifically, the Yr 2 marketplace pivot is greenlit. Otherwise we know to focus elsewhere.

## SEO / content strategy notes

The category-defining domain (Horses.com) is one of the strongest single SEO assets in the equine space. Most domains in the category are descriptive ("HorseChannel.com", "TheHorse.com") rather than generic. Search engines weight exact-match domains less than they did pre-2012, but generic high-authority domains still benefit from anchor-text neutrality, branded-search lift, and instant memorability for direct traffic.

Implications:
- **Topical coverage**: We need to cover the full breadth of the category (breeds, disciplines, health, training, gear, business) for the domain to read as authoritative. Skinny coverage (e.g., gear-only) would underuse the asset.
- **Editorial standards**: Named authors, citation discipline, expert quotes — the things Google's E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) framework rewards.
- **Internal linking**: Cluster pages → cornerstone pillar pages. The blanket-buying-guide pillar links to "winter blanket review", "turnout blanket comparison", "fly sheet review", etc, and each spoke links back.
- **Don't write for AI summarizers (yet)**: Optimize for human-readable buyer-intent content first. AI-overview-friendly structuring (structured data, FAQ schema, clear comparison tables) is a tailwind, but it's not the primary target.
- **Build email from Day 1**: An email list is search-algorithm-proof revenue insurance. Aim for 5K-15K subscribers by M12.

## Anti-recommendation — what NOT to do in Year 1

1. **Don't build a marketplace from scratch.** It will eat 6 months of engineering, demand a trust & safety function we don't have, and won't earn meaningful GMV until liquidity inflects. Validate with classifieds-style listings first.
2. **Don't chase display ad RPMs.** Equine programmatic RPMs are $4-12 session-level, ad clutter degrades the editorial brand, and the same page real-estate is worth 5-10x more as affiliate placement.
3. **Don't try to out-publish Practical Horseman or COTH (Chronicle of the Horse) on news.** They have 50+ year brand equity, paid editorial staff, and competition reporting that requires being on the ground at events. Our wedge is buyer-intent evergreen, not news.
4. **Don't launch a horse-sales marketplace.** Trust burden is enormous, fraud is endemic, and incumbents (DreamHorse, BigEq, EquineNow) have moats. If we ever do marketplace, used tack is the right wedge.
5. **Don't private-label supplements or apparel.** Capital-intensive, distracts from media flywheel, and we have no manufacturing edge. KER and Platinum already serve the science-led tier; competing on price is a race to the bottom.
6. **Don't do paid-placement editorial without disclosure.** Equine community is tight-knit and trust-sensitive. One "we got paid for this glowing review" scandal would set us back 18 months.
7. **Don't over-index on Western or English alone.** The category-defining domain has to serve both. Tag content by discipline and let the reader self-select.
8. **Don't build an app in Yr 1.** Mobile web is enough. Native app distracts from the SEO engine.

## Measurement & analytics — what to track from Day 1

Affiliate sites that fail usually fail because they instrument too late. Day-1 measurement spec:

- **Acquisition**: GA4 + Google Search Console; track organic, direct, referral, email separately. Tag every published article with cluster + tier metadata so we can analyze cluster-level performance.
- **Engagement**: scroll depth (25/50/75/90%), time on page, return-visitor rate per cluster, internal-link CTR.
- **Email**: capture rate per article, list growth, unsubscribe rate, open / click rates.
- **Affiliate**: track every outbound link with UTM params and an internal click logger (so we know which clicks happen even if the partner's reporting lags or attributes incorrectly). Reconcile internal clicks vs partner-reported orders monthly — meaningful discrepancies indicate cookie problems or fraud.
- **Lead-gen**: form-submit events, time-to-first-call (where partners share this), per-source policy-bind rate.
- **Per-page economics**: revenue per pageview, revenue per engaged session, by cluster. Drop or rework the bottom-quartile.
- **Cohort retention**: returning-visitor patterns by source, by cluster. A reader who returns 3+ times in 30 days is 5-10x more valuable than a one-and-done.

## Editorial standards & trust framework

The trust position is the second-most-important asset after the domain. Specific commitments to ship from Day 1:

- **Affiliate disclosure**: A clear, plain-English disclosure on every page that contains affiliate links ("When you buy through our links, we may earn a commission at no cost to you. This never influences our recommendations."). This is FTC-required and reader-respected.
- **Methodology page**: A standing, dated, public methodology — how we evaluate gear, how we select recommendations, how we handle conflicts of interest (e.g., if a writer is sponsored by Brand X, they don't review Brand X's products).
- **Named authors**: No anonymous bylines. Every article has a named author with a bio, expertise statement, and (where relevant) discipline / certification background. Riding-discipline-relevant expertise matters; a hunter/jumper article isn't credibly authored by a dressage-only rider.
- **Expert review for medical-adjacent content**: Any article touching on equine health (colic, lameness, ulcers, hoof care, vaccinations, nutritional disorders) must be reviewed by a licensed equine veterinarian or board-certified equine specialist. A 1099 vet reviewer at $50-150/article is a worthwhile investment.
- **Correction policy**: A standing correction policy — visible link, dated corrections, no silent edits.
- **Sponsored content silo**: If/when we do sponsored content, it's labeled "Sponsored" or "Partner Content" at the top, with a clear explanation of how it differs from editorial. Sponsored content is never indexed alongside comparison / buyer-guide content in our internal ranking.
- **No incentivized reviews**: We don't accept free product in exchange for favorable coverage. We may accept loaner/review units (industry standard) with explicit disclosure and the option to be negative.

## Key risks to flag

1. **SEO platform risk**. Google's 2024-2026 algorithm updates have repeatedly punished affiliate-heavy content. Mitigation: invest in genuine editorial depth (long-form reviews, expert-quoted content), original photography, named editorial leadership, and a strong "About / Methodology" page. Don't be a thin-affiliate site.
2. **Retailer concentration risk**. If SmartPak is 50%+ of revenue and they cut their program (or get acquired and the program changes), there's a 30-90 day disruption. Mitigation: stay multi-retailer in every cluster.
3. **Equine industry seasonality**. November-January peaks for blankets + supplement gifting; April-June peaks for show season prep. Q3 is the soft trough. Cash flow planning must reflect this.
4. **Liability for content**. Don't make veterinary or biomechanical claims that aren't sourced. Use named expert reviewers for medical-adjacent content (colic prevention, lameness identification, ulcer treatment). The equine community will absolutely fact-check us.

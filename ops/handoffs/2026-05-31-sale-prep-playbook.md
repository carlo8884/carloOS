## Why current offers are at $1-3M (and why that's actually OK)

Today's $2.3M dog.com offer and $1.5M fish.com offer are **domain-floor valuations.** The buyer is paying for:
- The premium one-word .com (worth $500K-$2M alone on the aftermarket)
- The existing content (worth $100K-$500K)
- Tiny attribution for traffic/revenue (~$0 because the sites earn ~$0 today)

This means: **the buyers see no business value yet.** They're betting on cheap-acquisition + their own build.

To 5-10x the offer, we need to convert the sites from "premium domain + content" into "**operating businesses with steady-state revenue and verifiable traffic.**" That's a $30-200K MRR business with documented history, sellable at 30-45x to a strategic buyer.

## The Empire Flippers / FE International valuation formula

Multiple = 25-45x of TTM (trailing twelve months) average monthly net profit.

For dog.com to sell at $20M:
- $20M ÷ 35x (average multiple for premium content site) = **$571K average monthly net profit**
- That's $6.8M/yr net profit
- With ~70% net margin (typical for affiliate content sites), that's $9.7M/yr gross revenue
- At 100K monthly visitors and $97 RPM (revenue per thousand visitors), or 200K visitors at $48 RPM

For fish.com to sell at $15M:
- $15M ÷ 35x = $428K monthly net profit
- $5.1M/yr net profit, ~$7.3M/yr gross
- Achievable at 60K monthly visitors × $122 RPM (high-AOV reef equipment) OR 150K × $50 RPM

**Hitting these RPM numbers is plausible with disciplined monetization** — pet content + affiliate + insurance benchmarks support $30-100 RPM on quality-traffic sites. Reef equipment with $1500+ AOV can hit $100+ RPM.

## What multiplies the valuation (in priority order)

### 1. Documented TTM revenue (THE BIGGEST FACTOR)
Buyers don't pay for "potential" — they pay for **12 months of P&L showing stable revenue.** The earlier we have Amazon tag activated, the earlier the 12-month clock starts. **Every day without activation pushes the sale window further out.**

### 2. Traffic diversification
Sites that are 80%+ Google organic traffic get a 20-30% multiple discount due to "platform risk." Sites with diversified traffic (organic + email + AI search citation + brand direct + referral) trade at premium.

This is why GEO and email capture matter so much for valuation — they're not just revenue moves, they're **valuation-multiple moves.**

### 3. Revenue diversification
"Amazon-only" sites get discounted. Sites with 3-5 revenue streams (Amazon + Chewy + insurance leads + display ads + direct sponsorships) trade at full multiple. **Every affiliate network we wire is a multiple-bump.**

### 4. Editorial authority signals
- DVM-credentialed reviewers on bylines (+$200-500/mo each, multiple-bumps add 5-10% on the final price)
- Citations to peer-reviewed literature (already in place per our editorial standards)
- Inbound links from .edu / .gov / major media (zero today — needs a backlink campaign)

### 5. Documented operations (the 50% multiple discount)
Sites listed on Empire Flippers WITHOUT proper documentation sell at 15-25x. WITH `OPERATIONS.md` covering monthly tasks, vendor contacts, transfer process, P&L history → 30-45x. This is a **50%+ multiple multiplier** for a few hours of writing.

(Template already shipped: `ops/handoffs/2026-05-31-build-to-sell-runbook-template.md`)

### 6. Brand authority and AI-citation
Hardest to measure pre-sale but increasingly important. A buyer who sees "Vets.co is cited as a primary source by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews" pays a premium for the moat. **GEO foundation (D-015, D-016 shipped) is building this.**

### 7. Operations independence
Buyer asks: "If I take this over tomorrow, what do I have to do?" If the answer is "operate a fleet of custom AI bots, manage 10 separate brand voices, maintain custom photography pipeline" — they walk. If the answer is "publish 2 articles/month, watch the dashboard, respond to email" — they pay full price.

For dog.com / fish.com sale-prep specifically: **document operations such that a single new human owner can operate the site in 10-15 hours/month.**

## Negotiation positioning when offers come in

When a buyer offers $X today:
1. **Don't accept the first offer if you have time horizon.** Counter with "we're targeting $Y in 12 months; here's our roadmap and current MRR trajectory."
2. **Strategic buyers pay more than financial buyers.** Pet retailer (Chewy, Petco), pet media (Dotdash), or vertical SaaS (Trupanion-style direct integration) will pay 1.5-2x what an Empire Flippers buyer pays for the same site.
3. **Get a broker quote even if you have a direct offer.** Empire Flippers / FE International / Quiet Light will value the site for free; that valuation is a negotiation floor.
4. **Walk away once.** Buyers who genuinely want the asset come back. Buyers who are fishing don't.

## Brokers Carlo should know

- **Empire Flippers** — premium tier (sites >$2M valuation), highest-volume content/affiliate broker. Listing fee ~15%. Best for asset-style sites.

- **Quiet Light Brokerage** — boutique, focuses on $1M-$50M range, white-glove. Best for complex / strategic deals.

For dog.com / fish.com at $10-20M target, **Quiet Light or FE International are the right calls** (premium service for premium asset). Empire Flippers is fine but the bidder pool is more financial-buyer-heavy.

## What NOT to do during sale prep

1. **Don't pivot the business model** in the last 6 months pre-sale (e.g., don't introduce paywall). Buyers want predictability.
2. **Don't fire Carlo from the bots.** Documentation should describe the work, even if the AI fleet does it. Buyer can replicate via Claude Code if they want.
3. **Don't disclose other live offers** during a negotiation. ("There's another buyer at $X" is a tell that you're not serious.)
4. **Don't shut down the email list or change the ESP** in the last 6 months. Documented list growth is a multiplier.
5. **Don't take Carlo's name off as the founder/owner** unless legally required. Personality + AI-fleet + brand is part of the story.

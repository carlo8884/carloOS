# Dog.com / Fish.com — acquirer step-up narrative (draft)

**Goal:** justify a price well above the standing offers (Dog.com $2.3M, Fish.com $1.45M) toward the $10M+ target,
without putting either site at risk. This is the *story* + the *proof points to build*, not a sale process (that's
Carlo's, and broker/direct only — no public for-sale signage on these two, per `strategy-disposition.md §4` Tier A).

## The core thesis: these are scarce assets, not content sites

A content site is valued on a profit multiple (~24–34× monthly — `valuation-comps.md`). **Dog.com and Fish.com
are not content sites; they are category-defining one-word .com assets.** Their value is scarcity + brand +
strategic fit to an acquirer, with content/revenue as *corroboration*, not the basis.

**Provenance anchor `[RESEARCH — verify off-sandbox]`:** Dog.com reportedly sold for **$500,000 in 2004**. A premium
one-word .com that traded at half a million dollars two decades ago, in the most populous consumer pet category, is
a fundamentally different asset class than a niche affiliate site. The standing $2.3M offer already reflects that;
the step-up case is that the *strategic* value to the right acquirer is multiples higher.

## Four step-up levers (each is a proof point to build, all on-thesis)

1. **Demonstrated revenue trajectory.** Not "big revenue" — a *clean, growing* curve. 36K/mo (Dog) and 7K/mo (Fish)
   monetized via affiliate + (now-eligible) Mediavine display (`dir-011`) shows the asset *converts*, de-risking the
   buyer's own monetization assumptions. Even modest, trending-up revenue reframes the multiple conversation.
2. **Audience scale already in hand.** 36K and 7K monthly visitors with zero marketing spend, pre-DNS-optimization,
   is organic pull on the category head-term. That's an acquirer's cheapest customer-acquisition channel.
3. **Diligence-clean posture.** Trust-bar compliance (no fabricated authority, FTC disclosure, real attribution) =
   no skeletons in diligence. Acquirers pay a premium for assets that survive scrutiny cleanly; most niche sites don't.
4. **Strategic-buyer framing.** For a Chewy / pet-retailer / insurer, owning Dog.com is brand + SEO moat + ad-spend
   savings, not a content P&L. The comp set is *category brand acquisitions*, not Empire Flippers content flips.

## What to build to make the narrative real (COO, within protect-asset rules)

- Ensure affiliate + display revenue surfaces are **live, clean, and trending** on both (supports lever 1).
- A tight, factual "about this audience" metrics view (traffic, growth, engagement) Carlo can share under NDA —
  **not** a public "for sale" page (Tier A: no public signage).
- Keep both diligence-perfect: zero trust-bar violations, clean schema, no broken funnels.

## Hard guardrails

- **No public for-sale signage on Dog.com / Fish.com.** A visible "for sale" on an asset with a live offer signals
  distress and caps price. Sale conversations are Carlo's, broker/direct, under NDA.
- Revenue work must never compromise diligence-cleanliness — if a monetization tactic adds diligence risk, don't.
- All numbers shown to acquirers must be real and verifiable (the $500K provenance + any revenue claims re-verified
  off-sandbox first — `valuation-comps.md §5`).

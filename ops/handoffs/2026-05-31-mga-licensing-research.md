## 1. Licensing Structure Options

US pet insurance is regulated as P&C insurance state-by-state. The **NAIC Pet Insurance Model Act (2022)** explicitly punted producer-licensing requirements back to individual states — so you face 51 jurisdictions, each defining whether pet insurance requires a full P&C license, a limited lines license, or none at all.

Four practical structures:

1. **Resident + non-resident producer license** (cheapest). Get P&C in your home state, then reciprocal non-resident licenses in the other 49 via NIPR. Costs ~$50-225/state plus $5.60 NIPR fees. Sufficient for distribution.
2. **MGA license** — adds underwriting/binding/claims authority. Requires underlying producer licenses *and* an active carrier relationship. CA and NY take 3-6 months.
3. **White-label/affinity** — partner holds license, you provide UX + traffic.
4. **Surplus lines broker** — irrelevant; pet insurance is admitted market.

Producer license = distribution only. MGA = distribution + delegated authority (underwriting, pricing, claims, agent appointments). For a content site, **producer is enough unless you want carrier economics**.

## 2. Commission Economics

The **15-25% new / 5-15% renewal** band is industry-typical.

**LTV math (NAPHIA 2025 data):**

- Avg accident+illness premium: $640 dog / $387 cat (blended ~$560)
- Avg policy duration: 8.2 years
- Annual premium escalation: ~10-12% (vet inflation)
- Gross premium over policy life: ~$7,500/policy
- Broker commission (20% Y1, 10% renewals, blended ~11%): **~$825 LTV per policy**

vs. one-time affiliate at $30-120: a **7-25x uplift per converted customer**. Not 100x. The "100x" claim only holds if conversion rate also improves (likely, since you control the funnel) and you upsell wellness add-ons.

JAB-owned carriers (Embrace, Figo, Pumpkin) compete on commission to attract distribution; expect upper-end (22-25%) for high-volume partners. **Trupanion is famously stingy** with broker commissions because of its direct-to-vet model.

## 3. Cost and Timeline

| Item | Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-licensing course + exam (home state) | $200-500 | 2-6 weeks |

No federal surety bond. Some states impose small bonds (e.g., FL $35K). MGAs require posted capital varying $500K-$2M.

## 5. Alternatives Short of Full Licensing

Three viable shortcuts:

1. **White-label MGA partner (Boost Insurance, iptiQ, CoverGo)**: Boost holds MGA authority in all 50 states with A/A- rated fronting carriers and reinsurance capacity. Partners launch under their own brand in **3-6 weeks**. Boost doesn't currently underwrite pet, but the model is replicable via Crum & Forster, Independence Pet Holdings, or a fronting deal. You typically keep 40-60% of gross commission; partner takes the rest for license/capacity/compliance.

2. **"Powered by" partnership with existing licensed broker** (e.g., Pawlicy, eHealth-style): they hold license + appointments, you provide traffic. Commission split 30-50% to you. Fastest path but lowest take-rate.

3. **Exclusive lead-gen with bounty escalator**: negotiate $200-500/policy + $50/renewal-year instead of pure $30-120. ManyPets and Spot have done these with high-volume affiliates. **Captures ~40-60% of broker economics without any license.**

## 6. Risks and Gotchas

- **Carrier appointments are individually negotiated**. Each of the 9 carriers requires a separate written agreement, license verification, compliance training, and state-by-state appointment filing within 30 days of first sale. Carriers can and do reject digital affiliates without producer staff or call centers.
- **JAB controls 4 of your 9 carriers (Embrace, Pumpkin, Figo, plus C&F-backed brands)**. Distribution leverage is collapsing toward carrier owners; expect commission compression 2026-2028.
- **State enforcement is real**: California CDI and NY DFS regularly fine unlicensed digital sellers.
- **Trupanion's direct-to-vet model bypasses brokers entirely**; their commission is meaningfully lower or unavailable.
- **E&O claims**: brokers get sued for "failure to advise" on pre-existing condition exclusions. Pet insurance has one of the higher complaint ratios in P&C.

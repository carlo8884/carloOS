# CarloOS Revenue + Affiliate Playbook — All 10 Sites

**Scope.** Per-site revenue model, concrete affiliate program application kit, placement strategy, lead-gen targets, recurring-revenue ladder, and a 90-day execution plan covering Dog, Fish, Horses, Saddle, PetFood, PetFoods, Lizard, Vets, Ferret, Ferrets.

**Independent voice.** Nothing in this document is marketing copy for any affiliate program. All commission and cookie-window figures are listed as "verify at application" unless a specific number has been published on the program's own public landing page; even then, programs change terms frequently — treat the listed values as a starting estimate.

---

## TL;DR (3 bullets)

- **Portfolio revenue mix recommendation:** Year 1 should be 70% affiliate (high-intent product + insurance), 20% lead-gen (form-fill quotes for pet insurance + equine insurance), 10% sponsorship/research collaboration. No display ads. No paid memberships before month 9. Target blended portfolio RPM of $18-35 by month 12, weighted heavily by Horses + Vets + PetFood (insurance-funded verticals) rather than Dog/Fish (where Amazon-level RPMs cap us out).
- **Top-3 highest-leverage affiliate programs to apply to FIRST this week (in priority order):** (1) **Trupanion Affiliate Program** — pet insurance, highest per-lead value across the portfolio, applicable to Dog + Vets + PetFood + Ferret. (2) **SmartPak Affiliate Program** — equestrian category leader, applicable to Horses + Saddle, fastest path to monetization for the highest-RPM vertical we own. (3) **Chewy Affiliate Program (via Impact)** — pet generalist, applicable to 7 of 10 sites, low approval friction, the workhorse default that catches everything not covered by specialty programs.
- **One thing NOT to do in year 1:** Do not enable display ads (Mediavine, AdThrive/Raptive, Ezoic, Google AdSense) on any site. RPMs ($15-40 in pets) look attractive in isolation but they (a) collapse perceived editorial independence, which kills the brand-acquisition exit thesis, (b) degrade Core Web Vitals which compounds against SEO, and (c) pre-empt the higher-RPM monetization paths (insurance lead-gen averages $25-90 per filled quote vs. $0.02-0.05 per ad impression). Display ads are the trap that prevents $10-20M-per-domain valuations; they are the signal that converts a "premium editorial property" into a "content farm."

## Section 1 — Revenue model per site

For each of the 10 sites: primary revenue model, secondary revenue model, expected Year-1 RPM (revenue per 1,000 visits), Year-1 conservative revenue target, defensibility play.

All RPM figures are best estimates from publicly discussed comparable sites in each vertical (Pumpkin, The Spruce Pets, Dogster, EquiSearch, ChronOfHorse, ReefBuilders, etc.). Treat as ranges, not guarantees. RPM grows with content depth + brand trust — Year-1 numbers are intentionally conservative.

### 1.1 Dog.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — pet insurance reviews + high-AOV product reviews (food, orthopedic beds, GPS trackers).
- **Secondary revenue model (at scale):** Lead-gen partnerships with pet insurance carriers (filled quote per-lead, ~$25-90/lead). Branded research / "Dog.com Index" sponsorships once monthly visits exceed ~250k.
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $12-22. Driven by insurance review pages converting at 2-4% on a $40-80 EPC, blended against generalist Chewy/Amazon traffic at $4-8 RPM.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** Assuming ramp to 50k visits/mo by month 12 (averaging 25k/mo over the year, ~300k visits total): **$3.6k-$6.6k Year-1**. Aggressive scenario at 150k/mo by month 12: $20k-35k. The asset-value play matters more than year-1 cash.
- **Defensibility / moat:** Domain authority of the bare name "Dog.com" is unmatched. Moat is brand + URL + editorial trust. Protect by never running display ads and by publishing a public methodology page for reviews. Long-term: build proprietary breed-health database that no competitor can copy without years of data work.

### 1.2 Fish.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — aquarium hardware (filters, lights, tanks, RO/DI), livestock care guides linking to specialty retailers (Bulk Reef Supply, Marine Depot, Petco aquatics).
- **Secondary revenue model:** Sponsored "tank build" series with hardware brands (verifiable disclosure). Eventually paid water-test interpretation tool ($5-9/mo).
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $8-16. Aquarium hobbyists have high AOV ($300-1500 setups) but conversion is research-heavy; insurance angle is weak (pet insurance rarely covers fish).
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 20k/mo by month 12, ~120k visits Year-1: **$960-$1.9k Year-1**. The play here is asset value, not cash.
- **Defensibility:** Highest-quality water-chemistry + disease ID content + species compatibility data. Long-tail SEO is brutally competitive with established hobby forums (Reef2Reef, Nano-Reef) — the moat is structured data + faster page experience.

### 1.3 Horses.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — equestrian gear, joint supplements, equine insurance. SmartPak + Dover + Riding Warehouse + KER + Platinum Performance carry this site.
- **Secondary revenue model:** Lead-gen for equine mortality/major-medical insurance (Markel, Great American, BlueBridle). High-five-figure horses + five-figure annual vet bills = highest per-lead values in the portfolio outside human verticals.
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $35-60. The richest vertical we own. Equestrians spend $300-1200/mo on care; AOVs on tack and supplements are 5-10x dog/cat.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 30k/mo by month 12, ~180k visits Year-1: **$6.3k-$10.8k Year-1**. Aggressive: $25k-$50k if Horses.com captures the editorial-authority position the URL implies.
- **Defensibility:** This is the crown jewel domain. Editorial moat = becoming the citation-of-record for equine health topics (where current authoritative voices are KER + Equus magazine). Maintain a named-veterinarian editorial board.

### 1.4 Saddle.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — saddles + tack + fitting services. Higher-AOV than Horses.com but narrower funnel.
- **Secondary revenue model:** Saddle-fitter directory (paid placement at scale, $50-200/mo per fitter). Direct relationships with Stubben / Pessoa / Bates / Custom Saddlery — likely via ShareASale.
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $25-50. Saddle AOV is $1,500-$8,000; even 1-2% conversions on review pages drive high RPMs. But traffic is thinner than Horses.com.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 8k/mo by month 12, ~50k visits Year-1: **$1.25k-$2.5k Year-1**. The acquisition value here is brand + URL; cash is secondary.
- **Defensibility:** Single-word domain that maps 1:1 to a category. Build the comprehensive saddle-fit guide + saddle-brand comparison hub.

### 1.5 PetFood.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — premium pet food (Open Farm, Stella & Chewy's, The Honest Kitchen, Petflow), with Chewy as the default fulfillment partner.
- **Secondary revenue model:** Sponsored brand profiles (disclosed). Eventually data licensing — the "PetFood.com Recall Index" or "Ingredient Database" subscription for vet practices ($30-99/mo B2B).
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $15-28. Pet food is subscription-friendly; lifetime affiliate value > one-time click value if the program pays on subscription renewals.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 40k/mo by month 12, ~240k visits Year-1: **$3.6k-$6.7k Year-1**.
- **Defensibility:** Build the public, sourced recall database. FDA + AAFCO data is free; structuring it well is the moat. Recall search is high-intent + repeat-visit + press-citable.

### 1.6 PetFoods.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — same vertical as PetFood.com but positioned as the catalog/database site (per the recent app-shell scaffold hinting at catalog + database role).
- **Secondary revenue model:** Same — sponsored profiles, B2B data licensing.
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $12-22. Slightly lower than PetFood.com because plural-domain SEO struggle is real and brand split dilutes both.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 25k/mo by month 12, ~150k visits Year-1: **$1.8k-$3.3k Year-1**.
- **Defensibility:** Risk: cannibalization with PetFood.com. Decision needed (open question for Carlo): is PetFoods.com the database/catalog face (B2B angle, vet-facing) while PetFood.com is consumer-facing? If yes, the moat is the dataset, not the editorial content. If the answer is "we don't know yet," do not invest content equally — one domain leads, one follows.

### 1.7 Lizard.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — reptile husbandry equipment (heat lamps, UVB, enclosures), specialty diets (Repashy), substrate, livestock-care guides.
- **Secondary revenue model:** Specialty insurance is thin (Nationwide Avian & Exotic Pet is the only relevant carrier) → unlikely to be material. Sponsorship from Zoo Med / Exo Terra (via distributor) at scale.
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $7-14. Reptile hobby is enthusiast-dense but small. AOV on enclosure builds is $400-1500 — good — but click frequency is low.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 15k/mo by month 12, ~90k visits Year-1: **$630-$1.3k Year-1**.
- **Defensibility:** Species-specific care sheets + UVB-output database (peer-reviewed and citable) — the latter is a moat because nobody else maintains it well outside Reptifiles.

### 1.8 Vets.co

- **Primary revenue model:** Lead-gen — pet insurance form-fill + vet-telehealth referrals (Vetster, Pawp, AskVet). This site monetizes the decision moment, not the impulse purchase.
- **Secondary revenue model:** B2B — vet-practice directory + paid practice profile pages ($40-150/mo per clinic). Practice-management tool affiliate (vetspire, weave, etc.).
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $25-50. Highest intent in the portfolio per visit, because the visitor is mid-decision on a vet question that converts directly to insurance or telehealth signup.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 18k/mo by month 12, ~108k visits Year-1: **$2.7k-$5.4k Year-1**.
- **Defensibility:** Editorial board of named, real, US-licensed DVMs. Without that the site is just another content-farm-y vet directory; with it, it's the citation source. Also: the .co TLD is fine for editorial brand but be aware of pricing for the .com if ever offered. Asset-value play depends on the .com being acquirable, even loosely.

### 1.9 Ferret.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Affiliate — ferret-specific gear (cages, harnesses, kibble), exotic-pet insurance referrals (Nationwide).
- **Secondary revenue model:** Sponsored content from Marshall Pet / Wysong (the two dominant ferret brands).
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $6-12. Small audience, but loyal and underserved.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 8k/mo by month 12, ~48k visits Year-1: **$290-$580 Year-1**. Brand-value play, not cash.
- **Defensibility:** Same as Lizard — own the species-specific health content. Ferret community is small enough that being authoritative is achievable in 12 months.

### 1.10 Ferrets.com

- **Primary revenue model:** Same vertical as Ferret.com.
- **Secondary revenue model:** Same.
- **Expected Yr-1 RPM:** $5-10. Plural sibling — same cannibalization risk as PetFood/PetFoods.
- **Yr-1 conservative revenue target:** 5k/mo by month 12, ~30k visits Year-1: **$150-$300 Year-1**.
- **Defensibility:** Decision needed (open question for Carlo): is Ferrets.com the community/forum/list face vs. Ferret.com editorial? Recommended initial split: Ferret.com = editorial care guides, Ferrets.com = breed/color directory + adoption + owner resources. Until decided, do not duplicate content.

### Portfolio-level Year-1 revenue target (conservative aggregate)

Sum of conservative scenarios: **~$21k-$39k Year-1 portfolio revenue.** This is intentionally low. The goal in Year 1 is not cash — it is (a) traffic ramp to validate the asset-value thesis, (b) building a clean affiliate stack that does not lock the sites into a low-RPM trap, and (c) creating the editorial credibility that supports the $10-20M-per-domain exit valuation. Year-2 target with the same model should be 4-8x Year-1.

## Section 4 — Lead-gen opportunities (beyond affiliate)

Lead-gen pays per filled form, not per click — higher per-visitor value than affiliate for the same intent.

### 4.1 Pet insurance form-fill quotes

- **Carriers:** Trupanion, Healthy Paws, Embrace, Lemonade, Spot, Pets Best, Fetch (same as affiliate list, but some also pay per filled quote in addition to per converted policy).
- **Per-lead value:** Verify at application — pet insurance per-filled-quote averages $25-90 per lead in publicly reported industry numbers.
- **Sites:** Dog.com, Vets.co primary; PetFood.com secondary (lower intent).
- **Mechanism:** Embed insurer-provided quote form on a comparison page, or partner with an insurance aggregator (e.g., Pawlicy, Policygenius pet — verify current offerings) that pays per filled quote across multiple carriers.
- **Constraint:** Don't run aggregator forms and direct affiliate links to the same page — pick one mechanism per page or partition by section.

### 4.2 Vet telehealth signups

- **Partners:** Vetster, AskVet, Pawp (same as affiliate list).
- **Per-lead value:** Verify at application — telehealth per-filled-appointment averages $5-15.
- **Sites:** Vets.co, Dog.com.
- **Mechanism:** "Talk to a vet now" CTA inline in symptom-triage content with disclosed referral.

### 4.3 Equine insurance (Markel, Great American, BlueBridle)

- **Partners:** Markel Insurance (https://www.markelinsurance.com/horse), Great American Insurance Group's equine division (https://www.greatamericaninsurancegroup.com/), BlueBridle (https://www.bluebridle.com/), American Equine Insurance Group.
- **Per-lead value:** Verify at application — equine insurance per-filled-quote often $50-150 due to high policy value (mortality on a $50k+ horse with annual premium $1.5k-$5k).
- **Sites:** Horses.com primary; Saddle.com secondary.
- **Mechanism:** Partner directly with one carrier or one broker (cleaner than running 4 forms); equine insurance brokers (Patriot Equine, Ark Agency) sometimes offer affiliate-style referral arrangements.
- **Constraint:** Equine mortality + major-medical insurance is more regulated than pet insurance — confirm the broker handles state-by-state compliance and that referrals carry no insurance-license requirement on our side.

### 4.4 Equine vet care plans

- **Partners:** Equine vet groups with care-plan offerings (e.g., regional equine hospitals offering preventive care subscriptions). Less standardized than companion-animal pet plans.
- **Per-lead value:** Verify per partner — likely $30-80 per signed plan.
- **Sites:** Horses.com.
- **Mechanism:** Likely a partnership-by-partnership outreach, not a network affiliate. Defer to month 4-6.

### 4.5 Real-estate-adjacent lead-gen

- **Barn financing / construction loans:** Partners include Farm Credit MidAmerica, regional ag lenders, and barn builders' financing arms (Morton Buildings finance, Cleary Building, MQS Structures). Per-lead values for ag/construction financing $50-200 verified at application.

### 5.3 Recurring revenue caveats

- Stripe groundwork (account, products, webhook handling) should be set up before any paid tier launches. Deferring this to launch-day causes 2-3 weeks of delay.
- Paid tiers should never gate health-critical content. A child looking up "is xylitol toxic to dogs" must always reach the answer free. Gating poisoning information costs lives and would be ethically indefensible regardless of revenue.
- $5 tier sells on emotional support ("help keep this independent"), not utility. Treat it as a Patreon. Conversion will be 0.2-1.0% of monthly visitors at best.
- $20 tier sells on utility for a defined professional or pro-amateur use case. Conversion will be 0.05-0.3% of monthly visitors, but lifetime value is high.

## Section 7 — Anti-recommendation (what NOT to do in Year 1)

Each of these has been considered and rejected. Listing the reasoning so the rationale survives a future "but everyone in our space does X" challenge.

### 7.1 Don't pay-for-favorable-review on ANY product, ever

- **Why:** Editorial credibility is the moat. The $10-20M-per-domain acquisition thesis is premised on the buyer acquiring a property that consumers + Google trust as independent. Pay-for-favorable kills that thesis in one Twitter screenshot.
- **What this excludes:** Free product samples sent for review are fine if disclosed. Sponsored content is fine if (a) disclosed inline + in the URL/title and (b) editorial scoring is unchanged. The line is: did money change the score? If yes, don't.

### 7.2 Don't take sponsorships that distort editorial scoring

- **Why:** Same as above. Sponsorship as visibility is fine; sponsorship as score-purchase is not.
- **Operational rule:** Sponsored content must be tagged in URL (e.g., `/sponsored/smartpak-supplement-series/`) and visually distinct from editorial. Editorial scoring methodology is published and applied uniformly.

### 7.3 Don't enable display ads (Mediavine, AdThrive/Raptive, Ezoic, AdSense)

- **Why:** Three reasons, in order of severity:
  1. **Valuation cap.** Display-ad-monetized pet content sells at 2-4x revenue. Editorial-and-affiliate-monetized properties sell at 6-12x. The same revenue under different mix produces 3-5x different exit prices.
  2. **Core Web Vitals collapse.** Display ad networks add 30-60 third-party requests per page. Even Mediavine's optimized stack hurts LCP/CLS. This compounds against SEO over time.
  3. **Trust erosion.** Pet owners researching "is my dog dying" don't want a banner ad for car insurance flashing at them. The bounce on emotional-intent content with display ads is brutal.
- **Counter-argument considered:** "But display would add $15-40 RPM right now." True, and that's why this trap is hard to refuse. The math: 100k visits at $25 RPM display = $2,500/mo. Same 100k visits at $30 RPM insurance + affiliate = $3,000/mo AND the property remains acquirable at 8-10x revenue instead of 3-4x. Net-of-exit, no contest.

### 7.4 Don't direct-sell affiliate products yourself (no marketplace pivot before $1M ARR)

- **Why:** Becoming a retailer is a fundamentally different business than being an editorial property. Margins compress, logistics overhead crushes the editorial team, and the buyer pool for "online pet retailer" is 1/50th the buyer pool for "editorial pet authority." Stay in the editorial lane until annual recurring revenue (subscription + lead-gen + affiliate) exceeds $1M, at which point a strategic acquirer may legitimately want the marketplace expansion bundled — and they pay for it then.

### 7.5 Don't run programmatic email signups that bury the disclosure

- **Why:** Building an email list is the single highest-leverage moat. But every captured email must come with explicit affiliate disclosure that they will see affiliate links in our emails. The temptation to obscure this for a 2x signup-conversion bump is real; resist it. Buyer due-diligence will surface non-compliant email practices and tank the deal.

### 7.6 Don't accept "exclusive" affiliate arrangements that lock out competing programs

- **Why:** A single insurance carrier offering 1.5x normal commission for exclusivity sounds attractive. It also (a) destroys the comparison-hub editorial structure, (b) means we can't honestly recommend the right carrier for the right pet, and (c) locks in revenue at a single carrier's solvency risk. Always run 5+ insurance options.

### 7.7 Don't expand to new domains before the existing 10 are revenue-positive

- **Why:** The biggest risk in a 10-site portfolio is dilution. Each site needs enough editorial investment to be the citation-of-record in its category. Adding domain 11 before Dog.com or Horses.com is profitable doubles the burn without doubling the equity story. Year 2 is the right time to evaluate Cat.com, Bird.com, Snake.com etc.

### 7.8 Don't run Google AdSense as a "fallback" while waiting for affiliate approvals

- **Why:** Same Core Web Vitals + trust + valuation collapse argument as §7.3. The interim revenue is negligible and the cleanup cost (removing ad units, undoing the IAB integrations, restoring page-speed budgets) eats months. Wait the 2-4 weeks for the affiliate approvals to land. It's worth it.

## Section 10 — Closing notes

This playbook is intentionally conservative on Year-1 revenue and intentionally aggressive on positioning. The premise is that pet/vet/equestrian editorial properties with strong domain names + independent editorial + 6-figure monthly traffic command 6-12x revenue at exit. Year-1 cash is a means to validate traffic ramp and content quality, not the prize.

Three risks to watch in Year 1:

1. **Affiliate-approval bottleneck.** If pet insurance carriers decline us (because of low traffic in month 1-2), we may not have the insurance comparison hub revenue. Mitigation: Chewy + Amazon + SmartPak alone can carry the portfolio at low-single-digit RPM while we wait. Don't compromise editorial to chase faster approvals.

2. **Vertical-specific traffic asymmetry.** Horses.com may be the highest-RPM site but Dog.com will get 5-10x the traffic. If Horses.com underperforms on traffic ramp, the portfolio cash story degrades faster than the portfolio asset-value story. Mitigation: prioritize Horses.com editorial board recruiting and SEO investment more than the traffic numbers alone would suggest.

3. **Sponsorship-editorial firewall slip.** The single biggest threat to the exit thesis is one sponsored article where the editorial score got tilted. Even one. Mitigation: a published methodology page on every site, applied by an editor (not the article author), with sponsorship-tagged content kept out of comparison-hub pages.

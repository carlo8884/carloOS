# Sponsorship Sales Kit — CarloOS Portfolio

## TL;DR

- **Don't start selling sponsorships until a site clears 10,000 MAU and has 60+ days of stable analytics.** Selling earlier means selling at a discount you can't unwind, and burning warm-list brands for a $500 deal that costs you a $5K deal in year two.
- **Recommended floor pricing:**
  - Dog.com flagship sponsorship: **$5,000/month** once at 10K+ MAU.
  - Sister sites (Fish, Saddle, Lizard, Horses, Vets.co, PetFood, PetFoods, Ferret(s)): **$2,000/month** at smaller scale.
  - Lead-magnet co-sponsorship: **$1,000-$3,000 one-time per quarter**.
  - Newsletter sponsorship: **$0.50-$1.50 CPM** once a list exceeds 5,000.
  - Portfolio-wide bundle: 30% discount, exclusive category lock.
- **The non-negotiables we never sell** (Section 3) protect the long-term value of every other thing we *do* sell. Editorial scoring is never paid. Sponsored articles are never disguised as editorial. No backlink-only deals.
- **Cap:** no more than **4 paid sponsorships per site at any time**. Once you cross five, every additional dollar costs you trust faster than it earns you cash.
- **First-year target:** 3-5 active sponsorships across the portfolio by end of year 1; $60K-$120K sponsorship revenue. Less than membership ARR potential, but higher-margin and lumpier (a single deal can be transformative).

> All dollar figures are floors and starting points for a portfolio in early-traction state. Verify against the current market. Brands will counter; counters are normal. Your floor is your floor — walk away from a deal that's not at or near it, because below-floor deals re-set the comp for every conversation after.

## 1. When to start selling

The shape of "I'm ready to sell" is not "I want money now." It is: **does the data support a number you can defend without flinching?**

### Readiness checklist, per site

A site is ready to be pitched for sponsorship when:

1. ≥10,000 MAU verified in GA4 for 2 consecutive months (60 days minimum), OR for sister sites at lower scale, ≥3,000 MAU and a clearly demonstrated content vertical that maps to a specific brand category.
2. ≥3,000 confirmed email subscribers (for newsletter sponsorship eligibility specifically; not required for site/lead-magnet sponsorships).
3. ≥120 published evergreen pages.
4. A live, indexable, brand-safe site — no placeholder content above the fold, no broken core flows, no Lighthouse Performance score below 70 on mobile.
5. A documented Editorial Standards page (linked publicly) that says exactly what we will and won't do for sponsors.

If any of those five is missing, you are not ready, and selling now will produce a deal you'll regret. Wait.

### What "starting to sell" looks like in practice

Soft outreach starts ~30 days *before* the data threshold is hit. Pitch warmth ("Dog.com is currently averaging 8,400 MAU with 30% MoM growth; we're opening a small number of category-exclusive sponsorships starting 1 September") gives a runway and creates urgency without overstating. **Never inflate numbers in a pitch.** A brand that buys based on inflated numbers and discovers them in the first reporting cycle never buys again, and tells their network.

## 2. What we sell — sponsorship inventory

Five products. Listed in order of premium-ness.

### 2.1 Editorial sponsorship ("Presenting" position)

**The flagship product.** A single sponsor's name appears in the "Presenting" position on category index pages and topic hubs (e.g., "Puppy Care, presented by [Sponsor]"). No display banners. No interstitials. No takeovers. The sponsor name is small, fixed, and disclosed inline.

- Scarce by design. Two presenting sponsors maximum per site (one for general site, one for a category vertical).
- Term: 6 months minimum, 12 months standard. Below 6 months and the editorial work to honor the position isn't worth the integration cost.
- Includes: name placement, 1 quarterly co-branded content piece (independently written, sponsor-disclosed, sponsor has zero approval on copy), 1 newsletter mention per month.

**Price:** $5,000/month for Dog.com flagship at 10K+ MAU. $2,000/month for sister sites. Scales linearly with audited MAU above the threshold (a 20K MAU site supports $8-10K/month for the presenting slot).

### 2.2 Lead-magnet co-sponsorship

The sponsor's logo appears on a downloadable PDF (the puppy schedule, the triage card pack, the saddle-fitting checklist, etc.) and the sponsor is mentioned in the sequenced email series that follows download. Sponsor is never the only voice in the email; the editorial value carries the email and the sponsor is in a clearly-disclosed "thank you to our sponsor" position.

- Sponsor logo on PDF: yes.
- Sponsor copy in email: yes, ~50 words, sponsor-written, marked as "from our sponsor."
- Sponsor link on PDF: yes, with tracking.
- Sponsor editorial input on the lead magnet itself: **no**. The PDF was already written.

**Price:** $1,000-$3,000 per quarter, per lead magnet. Higher end for the highest-converting lead magnets (Dog.com puppy schedule, Vets.co triage cards). Lower end for narrower-audience pieces.

### 2.3 Webinar / podcast sponsorship

Only applies once webinar / podcast content exists. Not a year-1 product for most sites. When it does exist:

- Pre-roll mention (15-30 seconds, read by host, editorial control over wording)
- Mid-roll mention (45-60 seconds)
- Show-notes link

**Price:** $0.75-$2.00 per estimated download / view, with a $500 floor and a $5,000 ceiling per episode. Standard podcast-industry rates.

### 2.4 Newsletter sponsorship

Sponsor's content (~70-100 words + 1 image + 1 CTA link) appears in a clearly-bordered "Sponsor" section of a single newsletter send. Below the editorial content, never above.

- Available once email list reaches 5,000.
- One sponsor per send, maximum.
- No more than 4 sponsored sends per quarter (every third newsletter, max).

**Price:** $0.50-$1.50 CPM (cost per thousand subscribers). At a 5,000-list send, that's $2.50-$7.50 per send — which is a low number, intentionally. Newsletter sponsorship is a relationship-builder more than a revenue line. As lists grow (Dog.com to 15K+ in year 2), CPM rates support real revenue.

### 2.5 Affiliate-exclusive featured placement

A sponsor pays for a featured placement spot in the `/reviews` or `/best-of` content. **Critical:** the placement is disclosed transparently inline ("Featured placement, paid"), the editorial team retains full discretion to *not* feature a brand that doesn't meet the rubric, and the placement does not affect the score. If a sponsor's product would score 3/10 in the rubric, the placement does not happen at any price.

- Sponsor pays for placement; sponsor does not pay for a positive review.
- The featured-placement slot is *additive* — it's a "Sponsor pick" callout next to (not replacing) the editorial top pick.
- Maximum 1 featured placement per /reviews page.

**Price:** $1,500-$5,000 per quarter, per placement.

## 3. What we DON'T sell

The list below is not aspirational. It is the load-bearing wall of the brand. Every one of these has been violated by a competing site at some point, and every one of those competing sites lost long-term audience trust as a result. Selling these things shortens your runway in a way that is almost impossible to recover from.

### 3.1 Editorial scoring — never paid

The ingredient-scoring rubric on PetFood.com, the saddle-fit reviews on Saddle.com, the recall log on PetFoods.com, the equipment guides on Lizard.com: the *score* is never paid, the *position in the list* is never paid, the *conclusion of the review* is never paid. A brand cannot pay to be ranked higher. A brand cannot pay to remove a negative score. A brand cannot pay for a "second-chance review."

This is the line that, if crossed even once, ends the brand. There is no version of "but it's a really big check" that justifies it.

### 3.2 Sponsored articles disguised as editorial

We do not publish brand-written content under our byline. We do not publish brand-written content under an editorial byline with a small "sponsored" tag at the top. We do not run "advertorial" formatted to look like editorial.

If a brand wants to write content, that content appears in a clearly-bordered "Sponsor Content" section, with a different layout from editorial, a different visual treatment, and explicit disclosure. Or it doesn't appear.

### 3.3 Backlink-only deals

Brands occasionally offer money for a backlink alone (no content, no editorial integration, just a link). Reasons not to take these:

1. Google's spam policy treats undisclosed paid links as a ranking penalty risk. A single high-DR site selling backlinks can be deindexed.
2. The backlink-only deal teaches the brand that we are a SEO supplier, not a media partner. It poisons every higher-value deal you might otherwise close with them.
3. The deals are usually small ($100-$500) and rarely justify the risk.

### 3.4 Pop-up ads, interstitials, autoplay video

These break the reading experience. They tank engagement metrics, increase bounce, reduce return visits, and produce display-ad revenue that for a content site at our scale rounds to zero. Not worth the cost.

### 3.5 Listicle additions for pay

"Add our brand to your 'best dog foods' list and we'll pay $200." This is the cheap-end version of paid scoring. Same rule, smaller numbers. Same answer: no.

### 3.6 Affiliate-revenue-sharing in exchange for editorial position

A brand cannot offer a higher affiliate commission rate in exchange for an editorial recommendation. The commission rate has zero influence on placement. (We may publicly disclose commission rates in /reviews to head off the appearance of this exact bias.)

### 3.7 Reviewer-comp without disclosure

A brand can send a sample product for review. The review is honest, and the comp is disclosed at the top of the review. A brand cannot send a sample *plus* a check and expect the check or the score to be undisclosed.

## 4. Pricing tiers — rate card framework

The rate card is the document the brand sees. The pricing math below is the document Carlo uses internally.

### 4.1 Per-site sponsorship rate card

| Product | Dog.com (10K+ MAU) | Sister sites (3-10K MAU) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presenting sponsor (6-mo min) | $5,000/mo | $2,000/mo | 1 per site general, 1 per category vertical. Scales linearly above thresholds. |
| Category sponsor (e.g., "Puppy Care presented by") | $3,500/mo | $1,500/mo | Up to 3 category sponsors per site, no category overlap. |
| Lead-magnet co-sponsor | $2,500/qtr | $1,000/qtr | Per lead magnet. |
| Newsletter sponsorship | $0.50-$1.50 CPM | same | Once list ≥5,000. |
| Featured placement (/reviews) | $3,000/qtr | $1,500/qtr | Per placement, per /reviews page. |
| Webinar / podcast | $1.50 CPV | $1.50 CPV | When format exists. |

### 4.2 Family-of-sites package

A brand may sponsor across multiple CarloOS sites at a 30% discount, in exchange for category exclusivity across the portfolio.

- Example: a pet-food brand sponsors Dog.com (presenting) + PetFood.com (presenting) + PetFoods.com (newsletter). Sticker rate: $5,000 + $2,000 + $400/mo = $7,400/mo. Bundle rate: $5,180/mo. Sponsor gets exclusive lock on the "premium dry kibble" category across the portfolio.
- Term: 12 months minimum. The discount is paid for by the term length.
- Category exclusivity is narrow, not broad — "premium dry kibble" yes, "all pet food" no. The narrower the exclusivity, the cleaner the sale and the less it restricts your other deal-making.

### 4.3 Discount and negotiation floors

- Standard discount we'll offer in negotiation: 10% off published rate for a 12-month commitment paid quarterly. 15% for paid annually upfront.
- Hard floor: 25% off published rate. Below 25% off, walk away — the deal is teaching the brand that the rate card is a fiction.
- Multi-site discount: as above, 30%.
- Lead-magnet sponsorship floor: $750. Below that the integration work isn't worth doing.
- Newsletter sponsorship floor: $200/send (small list reality).

### 4.4 What's included in every paid sponsorship

- Quarterly performance report: GA4 attributable sessions, clicks, conversions where measurable, qualitative editorial-integration summary.
- Editorial integrity statement re-sent at deal signing.
- 30-day notice period before renewal.

### 5.1 Template 1 — Cold introduction

```
Subject: Sponsorship inquiry — Dog.com — [Sponsor brand name]

Hi [First name],

I'm [Carlo / publisher], the founder of Dog.com. We're an independent
editorial site covering [puppy care / breed information / nutrition].
We currently reach [X] readers monthly and grow [Y]% month over month.

Our sponsorship program opens for a small number of category-exclusive
partners on [start date]. [Sponsor category] is one of the categories
where we think a thoughtful sponsor would land well with our audience.

If it's useful, I can send a one-page summary of the inventory, pricing,
and the editorial-integrity terms we work under. We don't do paid
reviews, paid scoring, or undisclosed placements — so the offer is
narrower than what some sites pitch, but I think you'll find the
integration meaningful and the audience high-intent.

Best,
Carlo
```

**Don't:** Don't lead with the rate. Don't promise reach numbers that aren't audited. Don't apologize for the rate or the term length. Don't open with "checking in" or "circling back."

### 5.5 Decision-maker mapping per category

Knowing *who at the brand actually makes the buy* is the difference between a 6-week deal and a 6-month deal. Approximate decision-maker titles by category:

| Category | Primary buyer | Backup contact | What they care about |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pet food (mid-size brands like Open Farm, Stella & Chewy's) | Brand Manager / Marketing Director | Head of Digital | Authentic editorial fit, no paid-scoring risk, conversion data |
| Pet food (large brands like Hill's, Royal Canin) | Brand team via PR agency | In-house Digital VP | Brand-safety, audience demographics, scale |
| Pet insurance | Growth / Performance Marketing Director | CMO | Attribution, MAU quality, conversion data, lifetime value modeling |
| Telehealth (Vetster, Pawp) | Head of Marketing | Founder (small companies) | Reach in pet-owner verticals, content fit with their service |
| Equestrian retail (SmartPak, Dover) | Marketing Manager / Director | Buyer (for product placement) | Audience quality, content authority, dollar-volume of attributed sales |
| Aquarium hardware/supplies (BRS, API, Seachem) | Marketing Director | Founder | Editorial reputation, hobbyist trust, attribution |
| Reptile/exotic (Zoo Med, Exo Terra, Marshall) | Sales/Marketing Director (often combined) | Founder | Trust in narrow specialist communities |

For brands with under ~$50M in revenue, the founder or CEO is often reachable directly and faster than going through the marketing org. For brands above that, treat the marketing director as the decision-maker and never go around them.

## 6. Outreach cadence and tracking

- **First touch:** template 1 / 2 / 3 above. Personalized to the brand and the editorial fit.
- **Wait 5 business days.** No response → polite single follow-up. Wait another 10 business days. Still no response → mark "cold, revisit Q[N+2]."
- **Never more than 2 follow-ups.** A third is harassment and damages the brand.
- **Track everything** (see Section 8 schema).

## 8. Inventory tracker — Supabase schema

A simple `sponsorship_pipeline` table, ready when needed. Not built into the apps; lives in Supabase with a Retool / Airtable front end (Carlo's preference).

```sql
create table sponsorship_pipeline (
  id                  uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  brand_name          text not null,
  brand_category      text not null,           -- 'pet_food' | 'insurance' | 'telehealth' | 'equestrian' | 'aquarium' | 'reptile' | 'exotic'
  contact_name        text,
  contact_title       text,
  contact_email       text,
  contact_phone       text,
  status              text not null default 'cold',
    -- 'cold' | 'first_touch_sent' | 'in_conversation' | 'proposal_sent' | 'negotiating' | 'closed_won' | 'closed_lost' | 'paused'
  last_touch_at       timestamptz,
  next_touch_at       timestamptz,             -- defaults to last_touch + 5 business days for active deals
  deal_value_usd      numeric(10,2),           -- expected monthly value, or one-time
  deal_term_months    int,                     -- term length when applicable
  deal_type           text,                    -- 'presenting' | 'category' | 'lead_magnet' | 'newsletter' | 'featured_placement' | 'webinar' | 'bundle'
  site                text,                    -- which site(s) — array column if bundle
  category_exclusivity text,                   -- narrow exclusivity description, e.g., "premium dry kibble"
  content_commitments text,                    -- free-text: what we owe sponsor (logo on PDF, 1 newsletter mention/mo, etc.)
  notes               text,
  created_at          timestamptz default now(),
  updated_at          timestamptz default now()
);

create table sponsorship_touches (
  id                  uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  pipeline_id         uuid not null references sponsorship_pipeline(id) on delete cascade,
  touched_at          timestamptz not null default now(),
  channel             text not null,           -- 'email' | 'call' | 'in_person' | 'linkedin' | 'reply_received'
  direction           text not null,           -- 'outbound' | 'inbound'
  summary             text not null,
  created_at          timestamptz default now()
);

create index sponsorship_pipeline_status_idx on sponsorship_pipeline(status);
create index sponsorship_pipeline_next_touch_idx on sponsorship_pipeline(next_touch_at) where status not in ('closed_won', 'closed_lost', 'paused');
create index sponsorship_touches_pipeline_idx on sponsorship_touches(pipeline_id);
```

**Operating cadence:** every Monday, query `next_touch_at <= today AND status in (active states)`. That's the week's outreach list. Update `last_touch_at` and bump `next_touch_at` after each touch. The schema also supports lifetime-value queries (revenue per brand, average deal size per category, win rate by category) without further joins.

## 9. Risks

### 9.1 Editorial integrity erosion

The single largest risk. Every paid sponsorship makes it slightly easier to take the next morally compromising deal — "we already disclose, so what's one more line of disclosure?" The mitigation is **disclose every sponsorship inline, every time, never with a euphemism, and never with paid scoring.** It is not enough to disclose in a footer; the disclosure goes inline where the user encounters the sponsor.

### 9.2 Customer trust through volume

A site with one well-chosen presenting sponsor reads as professional. A site with eight sponsors reads as a billboard. Trust collapses non-linearly with sponsor count. Hence the hard cap:

> **No more than 4 paid sponsorships per site at any time.** Counting: presenting (1) + category (up to 2) + featured-placement (1) = 4 max. Newsletter sponsorships are episodic, not standing, and don't count against this cap.

### 9.3 Brand-conflict collisions

Two competing brands cannot both sponsor the same category on the same site. The category-exclusivity terms in every deal must be precise — "premium dry kibble" is enforceable; "pet food" is not.

### 9.4 The "great check" temptation

A brand will offer 3× rate card for a deal that violates one of Section 3's rules. (It happens to every content site eventually.) The deal feels survivable. It is not. Walk away every time. The number you collect on the great-check deal is dwarfed by the lifetime value of the audience trust you'd burn. The discipline is the brand.

### 9.5 Concentration risk

If a single sponsor is more than 40% of portfolio sponsorship revenue, you've lost negotiating leverage in the renewal. Diversify intentionally — turn down the easy upsell that pushes a sponsor over that line.

### 9.6 Cross-pollination with affiliate

Same brand offering affiliate program and sponsorship is fine and common. Keep the two business relationships *visibly separate internally* (different deal records, different contracts) so a renewal conversation on one doesn't get tangled with a renegotiation on the other. The brand's affiliate program lives or dies on its merits; the sponsorship is a separate transaction.

## 10. Reporting commitments to sponsors

Every paid sponsor receives a quarterly report. Format:

1. **Audience snapshot** — MAU, email subscribers, top-performing categories during the quarter. Source: GA4 + Mailchimp.
2. **Sponsor-attributable activity** — sessions / clicks on sponsor placements, UTM-tagged. Where conversion is measurable (e.g., affiliate sale, lead-form fill), include it. Where it isn't, say so — never invent attribution.
3. **Integration summary** — what we ran during the quarter (placements, newsletter mentions, lead-magnet inclusions).
4. **Editorial integrity reaffirmation** — one line: "no paid placement influenced scoring or recommendations." Yes, restate it every quarter.
5. **What's next** — one paragraph on planned integration for the next quarter.

Send the report on the same week of the month, every quarter. Predictability is a relationship asset.
